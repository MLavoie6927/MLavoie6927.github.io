# GPS Trap Roads — Official Source Register

**Prepared for Marc Lavoie · 2026-11-17 · ATLAS v1.1**

This register documents **30 named roads** represented by supplementary GPS-conflict overlays. All 30 have source links from government/road authorities. The atlas includes the previous 80 general locations, of which 30 were themselves source-backed, so some new trap-road profiles intentionally expand on earlier locations rather than indicating 30 distinct additional physical hazards.

**Critical qualifications:** A source documents an applicable *restriction or operational guidance*, NOT a verified GPS misrouting incident. Map pins and road traces are approximate, schematic, and NOT a routable centerline, milepost boundary, crossing, or diversion. Sources should be rechecked before use; no live updates. A source-backed authority advisory may not be a universal prohibition.

## Interpretations

- **Access ban** — classifies a generally prohibited road for commercial truck operations; exemptions and special permits can exist.
- **Length** — compares overall combination length against the stated threshold; make actual vehicle measurements.
- **KPRA** — California kingpin-to-rear-axle measurements must be obtained independently; trailer body length is not equivalent.
- **Weight / registration** — actual gross weight and registered weight are distinct inputs and restrictions may have exceptions.
- **Hazmat** — cargo classification, quantities, time of travel, permit and tunnel limits must be verified; do not infer a prohibition for all cargo.
- **Weather** — describes standing condition-dependent procedures, not any active closure.

## Approximation

Each `road-traps.json` item provides a metro/region anchor and a short illustrative trace connecting two coarse geographic points. **These points are not an official published geospatial road segment**. Never ingest them into turn-by-turn directions, location-based routing, or legal vehicle clearance validation. A production navigator would need licensed DOT/commercial-network centerlines, milepost segmentation, vehicle restrictions, timestamped updates and independent verification.
