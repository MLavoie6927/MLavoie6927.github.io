01 / Operating method

The FIELD troubleshooting loop

A fix is not complete because an error disappeared once. A help-desk technician needs to identify the affected layer, perform an authorized low-risk action, prove restoration, and leave a record another technician can reproduce.

F

Frame

Who, what asset, which service, where, when, exact error, impact, urgency, scope, and recent change?

I

Isolate

User, device, application, account, network, service, policy, or physical hardware—which layer fails first?

E

Examine

Compare a known-good path, collect timestamps and logs, and test alternatives before changing state.

L

Least-risk action

Use the smallest authorized reversible change. Protect data, security controls, and the rollback path.

D

Demonstrate

Repeat the original task, check an independent signal, confirm with the user, document, and monitor or escalate.

Impact

How much work is blocked?

Safety and security first, then broad outage, critical service, many users, one user, and routine request—adjusted for real business consequence.

Urgency

How quickly does impact grow?

Use deadlines, active exposure, loss of workaround, and cascading dependencies. Loudness alone does not establish priority.

Evidence

What would change the next action?

Capture the exact timestamp, error, asset, account, service, recent change, known-good comparison, and result of every material test.

03 / Escalation quality

Escalate a decision, not a mystery

The next team should receive enough evidence to continue immediately. “Still broken” is not an escalation package.

01

Impact and scope

Users, devices, locations, services, business process, workaround, safety or security effect, and time pressure.

02

Timeline and evidence

First known time, exact errors, correlation IDs, screenshots when appropriate, logs, recent changes, and known-good comparison.

03

Tests and changes

Hypotheses tested, commands or tools used, exact authorized changes, before-and-after result, and rollback state.

04

Requested decision

State which authority, access, specialist judgment, vendor action, or infrastructure change is needed next.

Immediate escalation

Safety hazard, suspected compromise, data loss, lost or stolen managed device, unauthorized access, exposed sensitive information, widespread outage, destructive recovery, electrical damage, swollen battery, or any action outside your authority.
