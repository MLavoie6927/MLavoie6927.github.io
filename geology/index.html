The geological method

Observation first. Interpretation second.

A strong geological explanation connects several independent clues, states what remains uncertain, and identifies what new measurement could change the conclusion.

01

Frame the question

Define the site, scale, time interval, decision, and consequences of being wrong.

02

Observe and map

Record rocks, structures, landforms, water, fossils, coordinates, orientation, and context.

03

Measure and sample

Collect representative, authorized samples and preserve field notes, labels, methods, and uncertainty.

04

Test alternatives

Compare competing histories against field relations, laboratory results, imagery, and geophysical data.

05

Decide and revisit

Communicate the supported interpretation, confidence, limits, risk, and next observation needed.

Deep time

Earth history is read in sequence and calibrated by clocks.

Relative dating orders events through superposition, cross-cutting relations, inclusions, and fossil succession. Radiometric methods provide numerical ages when the material, isotope system, and geological context are suitable.

~4.54–4.0 Ga

Hadean

Planet formation, differentiation, earliest crust, intense impacts.

4.0–2.5 Ga

Archean

Early continents, oceans, microbial life, changing atmosphere.

2.5 Ga–538.8 Ma

Proterozoic

Atmospheric oxygenation, supercontinents, complex life.

538.8–251.9 Ma

Paleozoic

Marine diversification, life on land, Pangaea assembly.

251.9–66 Ma

Mesozoic

Pangaea breakup, dinosaurs, expanding ocean basins.

66 Ma–present

Cenozoic

Mammal diversification, mountain building, cooling and ice ages.

Dates are rounded for learning. Formal boundaries and numerical ages are refined by the International Commission on Stratigraphy; use the current chart for professional work.

Geologic energy systems

Before the first watt, there is a geological system.

Energy geology is more than finding fuel. It asks how a source formed, where it accumulated, whether heat or material can move, what contains it, how it can be measured, and how extraction, storage, waste, water, induced change, and closure will be monitored.

Primary sourcesCoal · petroleum · natural gas · uranium · geothermal heat · natural hydrogen

Geology supplies stored chemical, nuclear, or thermal energy.

Energy carriers & storageElectricity · hydrogen · compressed air · thermal storage · pumped storage

These move or retain energy; some rely on underground formations but are not raw geologic energy sources.

Enabling materialsLithium · graphite · cobalt · nickel · copper · silicon · rare earth elements

Minerals make batteries, conductors, solar cells, motors, generators, and grid hardware possible.

01

Generate

Organic burial and maturation, radioactive decay, fissionable material, planetary heat, or rock-water reactions create the source.

02

Move & concentrate

Fluids migrate, minerals crystallize, heat follows gradients, and sedimentary or tectonic systems focus resources.

03

Store & seal

Reservoir pores, fractures, adsorbing rock, salt, structural traps, or ore bodies preserve a usable accumulation.

04

Measure

Maps, cores, wells, assays, logs, seismic data, temperature, pressure, flow, and chemistry test the system.

05

Develop & monitor

Engineering converts the resource while monitoring water, subsidence, seismicity, emissions, waste, and integrity.

06

Close & verify

Plugging, reclamation, long-term containment, financial responsibility, and transparent monitoring complete the lifecycle.

Organic carbon

Coal systems

+

Plant-rich peat is buried, compacted, and altered through increasing coal rank. A defensible assessment maps seam thickness, depth, continuity, structure, moisture, ash, sulfur, gas, heat value, groundwater, and ground stability.

Geologist asks
Is the seam continuous and of usable quality? What faults, water, gas, roof/floor conditions, and overburden matter?
Decision boundary
Coal occurrence does not establish a mineable reserve. Recovery, safety, law, community effects, waste, methane, water, reclamation, and economics are modifying factors.
Petroleum system

Oil, natural gas, oil sands & oil shale

+

Conventional oil and gas require organic-rich source rock, sufficient burial and thermal maturity, migration pathways, a porous-permeable reservoir, a seal, a trap, and correct timing. Tight rocks, shale, bitumen-rich sands, and kerogen-bearing oil shale differ in storage and recovery.

Geologist asks
Are source, maturity, charge, reservoir, seal, trap, pressure, fluid properties, and preservation all present?
Decision boundary
A seismic closure is a prospect—not proof of hydrocarbons. Drilling, logs, samples, pressure, flow, water, emissions, induced effects, and abandonment obligations must be evaluated.
Nuclear minerals

Uranium & thorium

+

Uranium can be concentrated by magmatic, hydrothermal, sedimentary, unconformity, and groundwater-redox processes. Thorium occurs in minerals such as monazite and thorite and is commonly associated with placer, alkaline igneous, carbonatite, pegmatite, and related systems.

Geologist asks
What mineral hosts the element? What controls grade, geometry, disequilibrium, radiation, groundwater behavior, and processing?
Decision boundary
Commercial reactors use uranium fuel. Thorium is a fertile material that can produce fissile uranium-233 in suitable fuel cycles; it is not simply a drop-in fuel. Mining, milling, tailings, transport, safeguards, licensing, and long-term waste require strict regulation.
Earth heat

Hydrothermal, enhanced & closed-loop geothermal

+

Conventional hydrothermal power needs useful heat, fluid, and permeability. Enhanced geothermal systems seek to create or improve fluid pathways in hot rock; closed-loop concepts circulate working fluid through sealed wells without relying on a permeable reservoir.

Geologist asks
What are temperature, depth, permeability, fracture orientation, stress, fluid chemistry, recharge, scaling, and sustainable heat extraction?
Decision boundary
High temperature alone is not a project. Drilling performance, circulation, induced seismicity, water, corrosion, mineral scaling, thermal decline, and monitoring govern feasibility and risk.
Emerging resource

Geologic hydrogen

+

Naturally occurring hydrogen can form through several water-rock and radiolytic processes, including reactions involving iron-rich rocks. A potential accumulation still needs generation, migration, reservoir, seal, preservation, and charge—an emerging “hydrogen system” analogous to petroleum-system reasoning.

Geologist asks
What reaction generates hydrogen, at what rate, and can a reservoir retain a small, reactive, diffusive molecule? Are associated gases or microbes consuming it?
Decision boundary
Prospectivity is not recoverability. Exploration is early and economic accumulations remain rare; lifecycle emissions, drilling, leakage, water, land, infrastructure, and end use must be measured before making low-carbon claims.
Subsurface flexibility

Geologic energy & carbon storage

+

Salt caverns, depleted reservoirs, mines, boreholes, and some aquifers may store chemical, mechanical, or thermal energy. Deep formations can also be assessed for carbon dioxide storage, where containment and monitoring—not energy generation—are the central geological questions.

Geologist asks
What are capacity, injectivity, caprock integrity, faults, pressure limits, geochemistry, legacy wells, groundwater separation, and monitoring signals?
Decision boundary
Volumetric capacity is not usable capacity. Site characterization, pore-space ownership, permits, well integrity, pressure management, induced seismicity, leakage pathways, and post-closure responsibility determine viability.

Minerals behind the grid

Renewable power is not geology-free.

Wind, solar, batteries, transmission, and electric motors depend on mineral supply chains. The exact materials vary by chemistry and design, so geology must connect deposit knowledge to processing, substitution, recycling, environmental performance, and secure supply.

Battery systems

Lithium, graphite, nickel, cobalt, manganese, phosphate, iron, copper, and aluminum may be used depending on cell chemistry.

Solar systems

Silicon, silver, copper, aluminum, tellurium, cadmium, indium, gallium, and other materials vary by photovoltaic technology.

Wind & motors

Steel, copper, aluminum, concrete minerals, and—in some permanent-magnet designs—neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium, or terbium.

Grid & storage

Copper and aluminum conduct power; battery, pumped, compressed-air, thermal, hydrogen, and underground storage options require different geology and materials.

Energy prospect decision gate

Do not confuse “present” with “producible.”

A resource decision should move only when evidence closes the gap from geological occurrence to a responsibly developable system.

  1. Resource: Is grade, temperature, pressure, volume, thickness, flow, chemistry, or heat sufficient—and with what uncertainty?
  2. Recoverability: Can wells, mines, processing, or circulation access the resource at useful rates?
  3. Containment: Do seals, faults, fractures, stress, groundwater, and legacy wells support safe control?
  4. Lifecycle: What water, waste, emissions, radiation, induced seismicity, land, transport, closure, and reclamation obligations exist?
  5. Decision: Stop, gather one decisive dataset, run a controlled pilot, redesign, or advance to qualified engineering and regulatory review.
