Federal / national government

U.S. civics overview. Offices, powers, and procedures vary by state and locality. Check current official sources for your address and issue.

In the U.S., the federal government is the national government. Its powers come from the Constitution. It is divided into three branches with checks and balances.

The three branches

BranchMain institutionsCore work
LegislativeCongress: House of Representatives and SenateMakes federal laws, controls appropriations, and conducts oversight.
ExecutivePresident, vice president, departments, and agenciesExecutes federal laws and administers national programs.
JudicialSupreme Court and lower federal courtsDecides federal cases and interprets federal law and the Constitution.

Federal law made under the Constitution can preempt conflicting state law. The Tenth Amendment reserves powers not delegated to the United States or prohibited to states to the states or the people. The exact boundary can depend on the issue and court decisions.

Sources: USAGov: branches of government; National Archives: Constitution; National Archives: Bill of Rights.
