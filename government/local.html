Local government and positions

U.S. civics overview. Offices, powers, and procedures vary by state and locality. Check current official sources for your address and issue.

Local government is the level people often meet first: roads, utilities, land use, libraries, emergency response, and schools may be handled by different local bodies. The Census Bureau classifies five types: county, municipal, township, special district, and school district governments.

Common positions

Position or bodyTypical role
Mayor or county executiveLeads local executive work where that form of government exists; the position may be elected or appointed.
City or county council / commissionAdopts local ordinances, budgets, and policy within state authority.
City or county managerAdministers day-to-day operations where the council–manager form is used.
Clerk, treasurer, assessor, sheriff, prosecutorHandles records, finance, property assessment, law enforcement, or prosecution where those offices exist; election and appointment rules vary.
School board and superintendentGovern and administer public school districts under state law.
Special district boardOversees a defined service such as water, fire protection, or transit.

How a local decision moves

  1. A resident, department, or elected official identifies a need.
  2. Staff and legal counsel review authority, cost, and options.
  3. The council, commission, or board considers the proposal in its public process.
  4. The adopted budget or ordinance guides implementation.
  5. Departments deliver the service; auditors, courts, voters, and oversight bodies provide accountability.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau: government organization; USAGov: state and local elections; USAGov: find elected officials.
