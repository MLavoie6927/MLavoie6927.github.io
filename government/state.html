State government

U.S. civics overview. Offices, powers, and procedures vary by state and locality. Check current official sources for your address and issue.

Each state has its own constitution. State law defines much of what counties, municipalities, school districts, and special districts may do. Titles and divisions of authority differ across states.

Typical state institutions

  • Governor and executive agencies: administer state laws and programs.
  • State legislature: makes state laws and appropriates state funds.
  • State courts: interpret state law and hear most ordinary civil and criminal cases.
  • Statewide and regional offices: may cover elections, transportation, public health, education, licensing, and environmental protection.

Connection to local government

States establish local government powers and many service requirements. They may distribute funds, set standards, and supervise programs while local bodies handle delivery. For a specific question, check both state law and the local charter or code.

Sources: USAGov: state and local elections; U.S. Census Bureau: local government types and state descriptions.
