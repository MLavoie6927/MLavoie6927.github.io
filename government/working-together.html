How local, state & federal governments work together

U.S. civics overview. Offices, powers, and procedures vary by state and locality. Check current official sources for your address and issue.

The levels have distinct legal authority, yet many public services rely on more than one. A federal law may set conditions or provide funding; a state may administer the program; a local agency may deliver the service. The exact arrangement varies.

Examples of shared work

IssueHow responsibilities can connect
Roads and transportationFederal funding and rules, state transportation planning, and local street management can all be involved.
Public healthFederal research or support, state health authority, and local health departments can coordinate.
DisastersLocal responders act first; states coordinate resources; federal assistance may follow applicable requests and rules.
EducationStates set major education rules; school districts operate schools; federal programs and civil rights law also apply.

Find the right office

  1. Identify the service or decision: street repair, school policy, state license, or federal benefit.
  2. Check the responsible agency’s official site and jurisdiction.
  3. For policy concerns, contact your relevant local board, state legislator, or members of Congress.
  4. For a service problem, contact the administering agency first and keep the case number.

Sources: USAGov: find elected officials; USAGov: federal branches; U.S. Census Bureau: local government organization.
