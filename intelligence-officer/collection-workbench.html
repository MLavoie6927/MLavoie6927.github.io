Operation Cascade Line · fictional exercise

One disruption, five connected systems

A severe cold front crosses a fictional region. Electric outages, degraded mobile service, low water pressure, delayed medical deliveries, and fuel-routing problems appear within two hours. The task is not to collect everything—it is to determine what the coordination group needs to do next.

Priority intelligence requirements

Translate the decision into answerable questions

A priority requirement should change a decision. Essential elements of information—EEIs—define the observable facts needed to answer it.

PIR 01 · Cause and scope

What is driving the service failures?

  • Which feeders, substations, towers, pumps, and routes are actually impaired?
  • Which failures are physical, which are communications blind spots, and which are supply constraints?
  • Where do field reports disagree with dashboards or public maps?

Decision link: Match generators and technical teams to the true constraint.

PIR 02 · Time to threshold

Which critical service loses safe operating margin first?

  • How many hours of generator fuel, battery, water storage, medicine, and staff coverage remain?
  • Which estimates use measured consumption instead of nameplate capacity?
  • Which facility has no workable substitute, bypass, or mutual-aid option?

Decision link: Prioritize by consequence and time remaining—not visibility or political pressure.

PIR 03 · Best intervention

Which action buys the most continuity?

  • Are generator voltage, phase, connector, fuel, transport, and operator requirements compatible?
  • Can one communications repair restore several telemetry-dependent services?
  • Which route, escort, access, or schedule change moves fuel and crews fastest?

Decision link: Select an action that removes a bottleneck across more than one system.

Requirement testIf an answer would not change priority, timing, ownership, or resource allocation, it is probably background information—not a priority intelligence requirement.

Competing explanations

Do not force one cause too early

Complex disruptions can have a primary cause, a visibility problem, and a recovery constraint at the same time. Test each explanation against supporting, inconsistent, and disconfirming evidence.

H1 · Physical damage

Weather damage is the primary driver

Would support: clustered feeder lockouts, field-confirmed line damage, blocked access, and restoration requiring repair rather than reset.

Would weaken: rapid restoration without field repair or outage reports that disappear when telemetry returns.

Initial treatment: Plausible lead explanation; still separate distribution damage from wider grid claims.

H2 · Visibility failure

Telecommunications loss is exaggerating the apparent scope

Would support: unreachable devices that remain locally operational, dashboard gaps, call-completion loss, and field reports that contradict stale maps.

Would weaken: independent field inspections and customer reports matching the outage picture.

Initial treatment: Likely explains uncertainty, but does not by itself explain confirmed feeder trips.

H3 · Recovery constraint

Fuel and logistics are slowing restoration

Would support: low generator fuel, missed tanker windows, blocked roads, crew staging delays, and parts or vehicle constraints.

Would weaken: adequate onsite endurance, open routes, confirmed deliveries, and restoration delays unrelated to supply.

Initial treatment: Better treated as a consequence amplifier than the original cause.

Synthetic evidence ledger

Separate observation, report, assessment, and unknown

Filter the records by system. A record is useful only when the analyst understands its origin, timing, access, independence, method, and limitation.

EV-01Observed · weather sensor

Peak gusts reached 62 mph along the western corridor.

Use: Supports hazardous conditions near the earliest failures.

Limit: One sensor does not establish damage at a specific asset.

EV-02Reported · utility dashboard

18,400 customer accounts were shown without service at 08:00.

Use: Establishes reported scope and trend.

Limit: Accounts are not people; map aggregation and updates may lag.

EV-03Reported · operator bulletin

Two transmission paths remained within normal operating limits.

Use: Weakens a region-wide generation or transmission explanation.

Limit: Does not show the condition of local distribution feeders.

EV-04Observed · dispatch extract

Seven distribution feeders were locked out after repeated faults.

Use: Supports physical distribution damage or persistent local faults.

Limit: Lockout status does not reveal damage location or repair duration.

EV-05Observed · network telemetry

Mobile call completion fell to 71% in three affected service areas.

Use: Supports a communications constraint affecting reports and coordination.

Limit: Does not prove every tower or data path is unavailable.

EV-06Observed · water telemetry

North-zone pressure sampled at 32 psi against a 55–65 psi seasonal baseline.

Use: Identifies a material deviation requiring direct operator confirmation.

Limit: Intermittent communications could make the sample incomplete or stale.

EV-07Reported · facilities engineer

A hospital generator showed 46% fuel and an estimated 18 hours at current load.

Use: Provides an endurance estimate for prioritization.

Limit: Consumption changes with load; the estimate needs trend validation.

EV-08Reported · carrier portal

A temperature-controlled medical delivery moved five hours past its planned arrival.

Use: Shows a health-logistics handoff under pressure.

Limit: An estimated arrival does not prove product loss or patient impact.

EV-09Reported · logistics coordinator

A grocery distribution center remained online, but four outbound routes were delayed.

Use: Distinguishes production availability from delivery access.

Limit: Four delayed routes need a denominator and destination inventory context.

EV-10Unverified · public posts

Twenty-eight posts repeated a claim that the water was contaminated.

Use: Identifies a public-information requirement and possible behavior effect.

Limit: Repetition is not independent confirmation; posts may share one origin.

EV-11Reported · preliminary laboratory result

Initial water samples were within the fictional utility's normal quality limits.

Use: Weakens the contamination claim for the sampled place and time.

Limit: Preliminary samples do not cover every location or future condition.

EV-12Reported · supplier dispatch

One fuel tanker could arrive at 11:30; a second was conditional on road access.

Use: Provides a candidate action window and a collection trigger.

Limit: A promised future movement is not a completed delivery.

Worked decision brief

Say what matters in ninety seconds

Synthetic first assessmentOperation Cascade LineModerate confidence · review at 11:30 or material change

Judgment

Physical damage to local distribution is the most likely primary cause of the outages. Telecommunications degradation is reducing visibility, while fuel and route constraints may determine which services fail next.

Why

High winds, repeated feeder lockouts, and normal transmission paths fit localized distribution damage better than a regional supply failure. Call-completion loss and intermittent telemetry explain conflicting dashboards but not the confirmed lockouts.

Impact

Water pressure, hospital generator endurance, communications-site runtime, and delivery access create the most immediate continuity risks. Current evidence does not establish water contamination or region-wide food shortage.

Action

Reserve generators pending compatibility checks, confirm the north-zone pump constraint, prioritize the first fuel delivery by measured time-to-threshold, and place one communications team where restored visibility would support several services.

Uncertainty

Field damage locations, actual fuel burn rates, and route completion remain incomplete. Several records are reported estimates rather than completed actions or direct measurements.

Change trigger

Reassess if field inspection disproves feeder damage, water pressure normalizes, a critical facility falls below eight hours of endurance, a tanker misses its window, or communications recovery materially changes the outage picture.

Analytic traps and review rubric

Grade the reasoning, not the confidence of the speaker

01

Dashboard literalism

A status map is a model of the system, not the system itself. Test update time, coverage, aggregation, and communications dependencies.

02

Missing denominator

Four delayed routes means something different out of five routes than out of 400.

03

Copied corroboration

Ten reports repeating one dispatch note are one origin, not ten independent confirmations.

04

Single-cause pressure

Primary cause, visibility failure, and recovery constraint can coexist and require different actions.

05

Absence as failure

Missing telemetry may mean communications loss, access limits, or delayed reporting—not asset failure.

06

Static confidence

Confidence should change when access, evidence quality, alternatives, or time changes.

After-action review questions

  1. Did the assessment answer the decision maker's actual choice and deadline?
  2. Were observations, reports, assessments, and unknowns labeled separately?
  3. Were alternative explanations tested with evidence that could disconfirm them?
  4. Were specialist limits, source dependence, missing denominators, and stale data recognized?
  5. Did the recommendation identify an owner, action, consequence, and review trigger?
  6. After the outcome, which indicator worked, which failed, and what should change in the model?
