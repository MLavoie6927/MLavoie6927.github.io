All examples are synthetic teaching scenarios. Real collection must use public, licensed, supplied, or properly authorized information and must be validated by current sources and qualified specialists.

The universal data request

Ask for meaning, not just a spreadsheet

A number without definition, denominator, place, time, provenance, and limitations can create false confidence. Put these requirements into every collection request.

01

Decision

Who must choose what, by when, and what observation would change that choice?

02

Definition

What exactly does each field measure, exclude, aggregate, or classify?

03

Unit & denominator

Is this a count, rate, percentage, duration, capacity, value, or estimate—and out of what?

04

Time & place

Which geography, population, asset, interval, timestamp, time zone, and reporting window apply?

05

Baseline

What is normal for this location, season, weekday, operating mode, and comparison group?

06

Provenance

Which system or person produced the data, by what method, and is another report merely a copy?

07

Quality

What is missing, delayed, revised, sampled, estimated, duplicated, biased, or affected by clock error?

08

Authority & handling

Who owns it, is access authorized, what privacy or safety limits apply, and who may receive it?

Collection formulaDecision + unit of analysis + baseline + changed handoff + discriminating evidence + lawful handling + review time.

Group 01 · Essential systems

Movement, supply, power, communications, water, and shelter

These systems carry the people, goods, energy, information, and basic services that other sectors depend on.

01Logistics & transportPorts · rail · trucking · aviation · warehousing · last mile

Fictional exampleA hospital network reports that temperature-controlled medicine deliveries are arriving two days late.

Priority questions

  1. Which product, shipment, route, carrier, handoff, and time window are affected?
  2. Did delay begin at the port, terminal, warehouse, line haul, or final delivery?
  3. How many days of usable inventory are already downstream, and which locations reach a decision threshold first?

Operational data requestPlanned and actual arrival times; port and warehouse dwell; missed pickups; route closures; carrier capacity; cold-chain temperatures; fuel, labor, and customs status; inventory cover; delivery exceptions; synchronized timestamps and shipment identifiers.

02Food & agricultureInputs · farms · processing · cold chain · retail

Fictional exampleSeveral stores show empty produce shelves while regional prices rise after severe weather.

Priority questions

  1. Is the problem production, processing, transport, food safety, retail replenishment, affordability, or unusual demand?
  2. Which crop, lot, supplier, facility, geography, and consumer group are affected?
  3. Are shelf images representative, and what independent records would disprove a regional shortage?

Operational data requestAcreage and yield estimates; input availability; plant uptime; cold-chain records; inbound and outbound volume; days of inventory; sales velocity; waste; recalls; wholesale and retail prices by location; substitute-product capacity.

03Energy & utilitiesGeneration · fuel · transmission · distribution · restoration

Fictional exampleA heat wave produces rolling outages and conflicting estimates for restoration.

Priority questions

  1. Is the constraint generation, fuel, transmission, distribution, market dispatch, or a local protection event?
  2. What load, customers, and critical facilities are affected, and which have working backup power?
  3. What restoration sequence is being followed, and why are estimated times changing?

Operational data requestDemand and generation by interval; reserve margin; generation mix; fuel inventory; outage footprint; feeder and substation status; frequency and voltage exceptions; protection alarms; crew work orders; critical-facility backup status; restoration-estimate history.

04TelecommunicationsFiber · mobile · satellite · spectrum · interconnection

Fictional exampleResidents report failed emergency calls while ordinary internet access remains partly available.

Priority questions

  1. Which carrier, service, geography, device class, and network layer are affected?
  2. Is failure caused by tower power, fiber, backhaul, signaling, routing, DNS, congestion, application, or customer equipment?
  3. Are emergency calls and public-safety communications affected differently from consumer traffic?

05Water & wastewaterSource · treatment · pumping · distribution · collection

Fictional exampleA pressure loss leads to a boil-water notice affecting one service zone.

Priority questions

  1. Is the concern quantity, pressure, quality, treatment, distribution, wastewater capacity, or customer plumbing?
  2. Which zone and customers are affected, and what laboratory or operational test confirms safety?
  3. How long can storage, chemicals, backup power, pumping, and alternate supply sustain service?

Operational data requestChlorine residual; turbidity; microbiology; pressure; flow; tank levels; pump and valve status; power and generator fuel; treatment-chemical inventory; break and work-order history; laboratory methods and chain of custody; service-zone maps; notice timing and clearance criteria.

06Housing, construction & land useLand · approvals · finance · labor · materials · inspection

Fictional exampleRents rise after a storm even though official unit counts show little population change.

Priority questions

  1. How many units are available, affordable, habitable, insured, accessible, and connected to utilities?
  2. Is pressure caused by damage, displacement, vacancy, permitting, labor, materials, financing, insurance, or land rules?
  3. Which household types and neighborhoods face the earliest loss of safe shelter?

Operational data requestVacancies; asking and paid rent; income burden; permits; starts; completions; damage and habitability inspections; utility connects; insurance claims; evictions; shelter use; zoning capacity; material prices and lead times; contractor availability; repair timelines.

Group 02 · Human systems

Health, education, population, culture, and influence

Human behavior and access determine how physical conditions become real outcomes for different communities.

07Health & public healthPrevention · diagnosis · care · medicines · surveillance

Fictional exampleEmergency departments report an unusual increase in respiratory complaints across three counties.

Priority questions

  1. What is the case definition, baseline, onset date, severity profile, and geographic distribution?
  2. Is the rise caused by disease, exposure, testing behavior, reporting change, or healthcare access?
  3. Which hospitals, staff, medicines, oxygen, beds, and referral pathways are approaching capacity?

Operational data requestSyndromic visits; tests and positivity; admissions; ICU use; length of stay; staffing; bed availability; medicine and oxygen inventory; age and geography; environmental exposure indicators; denominator populations; reporting lag; case-definition changes.

08Education & workforceSchools · training · credentials · labor pipelines

Fictional exampleRural schools report losing nurses and technical instructors faster than positions can be filled.

Priority questions

  1. Is the shortage an actual unfilled position, a funded vacancy, a posting, a credential gap, or a retention problem?
  2. Which training, licensing, compensation, location, workload, and housing constraints limit replacement?
  3. What student or community service is degraded, and how long until the pipeline can restore capacity?

Operational data requestAuthorized headcount and full-time equivalents; funded positions; vacancies; applicants; time to hire; turnover; compensation; workload; licensure; graduation and placement; enrollment; attendance; class size; service backlogs; geographic distribution; exit reasons.

09Demographics & migrationPopulation · age · households · movement · services

Fictional exampleSchool enrollment falls while housing demand and clinic visits rise in the same city.

Priority questions

  1. Are population, households, age structure, commuting, seasonal presence, or administrative coverage changing?
  2. Which movements are temporary, permanent, voluntary, forced, documented, or missing from official systems?
  3. Which services gain or lose demand, and which groups are invisible in the available denominator?

Operational data requestCensus and survey estimates; births and deaths; school enrollment; address changes; housing occupancy; utility activations; transit and commuting; clinic utilization; age and household composition; language needs; lawful aggregate mobility indicators; confidence intervals and undercount methods.

10Culture & languageMeaning · norms · identity · translation · trusted messengers

Fictional exampleAn emergency warning reaches most phones but produces little protective action in one community.

Priority questions

  1. How do local language, literacy, history, identity, norms, and trusted relationships change the message's meaning?
  2. Was the message translated accurately, delivered through an accessible channel, and tied to a practical action?
  3. What explanation fits nonresponse besides defiance—access, disability, work, transport, distrust, ambiguity, or prior false alarms?

Operational data requestMessage versions and translations; readability; timestamps; reach by channel; accessibility; hotline themes; consent-based interviews; community feedback; local media framing; trusted-messenger input; observed protective behavior; prior warning history; subgroup differences interpreted without stereotyping.

11People, organizations & influencePsychology · leadership · incentives · narratives · behavior

Fictional exampleA screenshot falsely says a storm shelter is closed and urges residents to use an unlisted site.

Priority questions

  1. What claim and behavior are being promoted, to which audience, through which trusted or vulnerable channel?
  2. What is the earliest traceable origin, and are later posts independent sources or copies?
  3. What observable effect occurred, and do error, satire, stale information, copying, or coordination fit better?

Operational data requestOriginal content, URL and hash; first-known timestamp; archive history; repost lineage; account and channel history; reach versus engagement; cross-platform timing; official status; correction timing; hotline or call-volume effects; lawful aggregate audience research. Repetition alone does not prove attribution.

Group 03 · Economic, industrial, and technical systems

Money, production, science, technology, and engineering

These sectors determine whether ideas, capital, materials, and technical capability become reliable outputs.

12Economics & financeOutput · prices · labor · credit · trade · public budgets

Fictional exampleLocal food and housing prices rise faster than national figures while wages remain flat.

Priority questions

  1. Is the signal nominal or inflation-adjusted, total or per person, local or national, temporary or structural?
  2. Is pressure driven by supply, demand, policy, market power, credit, expectations, or measurement change?
  3. Who gains, who pays, and through which market or policy mechanism does the effect travel?

Operational data requestLocal price baskets; wages; employment; hours; sales; inventory; vacancy; credit conditions; delinquency; trade and freight flow; household income and burden; business openings and closures; government budgets; methodology notes; seasonal adjustment and revision history.

13Banking & paymentsDeposits · credit · settlement · cards · cash · fraud controls

Fictional exampleCard declines surge across several merchants while customers can still view correct balances.

15Science & researchHypothesis · method · measurement · replication · review

Fictional exampleA widely shared headline claims that one study proves a new treatment improves outcomes.

Priority questions

  1. What endpoint was measured, against which comparison, in what sample, with what effect size and uncertainty?
  2. Was the analysis preregistered, peer reviewed, corrected, independently replicated, or contradicted?
  3. Does the study population and method support the public claim or the decision now being considered?

Operational data requestProtocol; preregistration; inclusion criteria; sample characteristics; control design; raw or summary data; effect sizes; confidence intervals; attrition; adverse events; code; conflicts; correction history; replication results; systematic reviews.

Group 04 · Governance, security, and strategic systems

Authority, emergencies, defense, diplomacy, and environment

These sectors connect lawful authority, public protection, national capability, international effects, and long-term physical conditions.

18Government & lawAuthority · policy · budget · administration · review

Fictional exampleA disaster-relief program is announced, but residents report that assistance has not arrived.

Priority questions

  1. Which institution has legal authority, funding, implementation responsibility, and review power?
  2. Is the action proposed, enacted, appropriated, regulated, contracted, operational, or still challenged?
  3. Who is eligible, what process reaches them, and where is the delay between announcement and service?

Operational data requestStatutory and regulatory text; effective dates; appropriations; agency guidance; eligibility rules; applications; approvals; awards; disbursements; contracts; staffing; service targets; geographic delivery; appeals; audits; court actions; meeting records.

19Security & emergency responsePrevention · preparedness · response · recovery · learning

Fictional exampleMultiple callers report a chemical odor near an industrial district and two schools.

  1. What happened, where, when, who is exposed, and which authority has incident command?
  2. Which observation would trigger evacuation, sheltering, or an all-clear?

22Environment & climate systemsAir · water · soil · ecosystems · hazards · adaptation

Fictional exampleA fish die-off appears downstream after hot weather and reports of an industrial discharge.

Priority questions

  1. What hazard, source, pathway, exposure, receptor, geography, and time window are being assessed?

From collection to judgment

Turn the data into a decision product

1

Label observations

Record what was directly measured, reported, inferred, unavailable, or copied from another origin.

2

Test alternatives

State at least two plausible explanations and identify the observation that would weaken each one.

3

Qualify confidence

Explain source access, independence, timeliness, data gaps, specialist limits, and why confidence is high, moderate, or low.

4

Brief the decision

Lead with the judgment, consequence, proportionate option, owner, and the trigger for reviewing the assessment.
