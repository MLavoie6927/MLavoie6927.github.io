Offensive PSYOP mission · high-level doctrine

The function exists—but it is governed, authorized, and assessed

Public Army doctrine describes Psychological Operations forces conducting military information support operations to influence approved foreign audiences in support of a commander's objectives. That is not a private “handler” independently controlling people; it is a mission activity bounded by authority, planning, approval, coordination, and assessment.

01Authority

Establish lawful mission scope

Identify the legal authority, command relationship, approved program, audience limitations, oversight, and decision rights before activity begins.

02Objective

Define observable behavior

State the authorized behavior or decision relevant to the mission, the conditions, the time horizon, and how success or failure can be observed.

03Audience analysis

Understand context and uncertainty

Analyze current behavior, causes, environment, culture, information access, and competing explanations while stating limits and forecast uncertainty.

04Integration

Coordinate approved actions

Align authorized activity with the larger operation, other government functions, partners, timing, attribution rules, and themes that are approved or prohibited.

05Delivery

Use approved sources and channels

Employ only authorized products, actions, sources, attribution, and dissemination channels under the designated review and approval process.

06Assessment

Measure effects and terminate

Compare observable behavior with the baseline, separate operational effects from outside causes, report uncertainty, and change or stop activity when required.

Governance stack

What must exist above an operation

  • Specific authority and approved mission
  • Approved audience and psychological objective
  • Legal, policy, intelligence, cultural, and operational review
  • Themes to stress and avoid
  • Approved sources, channels, attribution, and dissemination
  • Assessment criteria, reporting, review, and termination conditions
Portfolio boundary

The public page stops at doctrine, governance, analysis, and defensive detection. It does not provide individualized vulnerability exploitation, covert persona construction, manipulation scripts, proxy tasking, evasion, or instructions for surrounding a real person with fabricated consensus.

Doctrine check · what the model is and is not

The six directions are an original defensive adaptation

Public U.S. Army material describes an eight-step Target Audience Analysis Model. It also treats behavior forecasts as uncertain and subject to continuing assessment. It does not establish a universal “four-to-six-direction matrix of control.”

Official concept

Eight-step TAAM

Army TM 3-53.11 describes an eight-step target-audience analysis process and a living worksheet that is updated as the environment changes.

Official caution

Forecasts are not absolute

The same manual says behavior forecasts have varying accuracy and should communicate uncertainty, limitations, and expected accuracy.

Portfolio model

Six-direction convergence

This page groups six common exposure directions so a defensive analyst can see how apparently separate pressures may reinforce one another.

Defensive abuse analysis · six-domain matrix

The Six-Domain Coercive-Control Matrix

This matrix examines how danger, betrayal, relational abuse, social invalidation, and economic pressure can converge around a person and narrow their real or perceived choices. It is a framework for recognizing harm, testing coordination claims, preserving evidence, and restoring agency—not a sequence for producing trauma.

Descriptive, not sequentialNo domain is required

Serious abuse may exist in one domain, several domains may overlap, and similar events may have unrelated causes. An acute threat is not an “arm” to apply. It is a safety event that may be accidental, independently criminal, or exploited by an abuser. Document the harm first; treat central coordination as a hypothesis until evidence establishes it.

Domain 01 · Safety

Acute threat and traumatic shock

Observe A serious accident, assault, medical emergency, disaster, or other event that creates a credible fear of death or catastrophic harm.

Test Establish what occurred, who had knowledge or opportunity, whether the danger was created or exploited, and what records or witnesses independently support the account.

Protect Address immediate safety and health needs, preserve incident evidence, and avoid attributing intent before the event itself is verified.

Domain 02 · Trust

Betrayal by a trusted person

Observe A friend or ally exposes private information, manufactures conflict, repeats unusual allegations, or withdraws support during a vulnerable period.

Test Preserve original communications and determine whether the person acted independently, misunderstood events, faced pressure, or received authenticated tasking.

Protect Rebuild a small network of independently verified support without automatically labeling every damaged relationship a proxy operation.

Domain 03 · Family

Family-system abuse

Observe Intimidation, scapegoating, conditional support, interference with care, threats involving children or housing, or control over transportation and documents.

Test Separate longstanding family patterns from newly coordinated behavior; compare wording, timing, resource control, communications, and independent motives.

Protect Identify safe contacts and independent access to housing, documents, transportation, care, and confidential assistance.

Domain 04 · Reality

Social and institutional invalidation

Observe Documented events are repeatedly denied, copied allegations appear independent, records conflict, or legitimate questions are dismissed without review.

Test Identify each speaker, source, record, and decision separately. Determine whether reports share one origin or whether policy, error, stigma, delay, or incomplete information explains the pattern.

Protect Create a dated evidence ledger and obtain neutral review of the underlying records instead of debating a vague claim that “society” is acting as one unit.

Domain 05 · Intimacy

Intimate-partner coercion

Observe Surveillance, isolation, threats, sexual coercion, control of devices or children, humiliation, alternating affection and punishment, or retaliation for independence.

Test Document the pattern, access to accounts and resources, disclosure of private information, connections to other actors, and any escalation after resistance.

Protect Use victim-led safety planning and private support channels; avoid confrontations or public accusations that could increase immediate danger.

Domain 06 · Independence

Economic and occupational control

Observe Withheld money, artificial debt, sabotaged employment, threatened termination, account lockout, damaged credentials, housing pressure, or benefit interference.

Test Review account records, contracts, payroll, schedules, evaluations, access logs, threats, and the lawful basis for employment or financial decisions.

Protect Restore independent access to money, identification, housing, transportation, benefits, professional records, and qualified legal or advocacy support.

01 Safety disrupted→ 02 Trust weakened→ 03 Support narrowed→ 04 Reality disputed→ 05 Intimacy weaponized→ 06 Exit constrained

This is one possible cumulative pattern, not a universal progression. Domains may occur in any order, overlap, or arise independently.

01 · Observed

Document the event

Record what happened, when, where, who directly observed it, and which original artifact supports it.

02 · Corroborated

Confirm independently

Determine whether a second source has independent knowledge or merely repeats the first account.

03 · Coordinated

Establish direction

Require tasking, shared control, nonpublic material, payment, synchronized adaptation, or another demonstrable link.

04 · Attributed

Name responsibility carefully

State actor, sponsor, intent, and confidence only when the evidence supports each separate judgment.

Analytic ruleObserved harm does not prove coordinated authorship. Coordination does not prove a single mastermind. Attribution does not prove that every participant was witting. Protect the affected person while testing each inference separately.
Defensive objective

Restore physical safety, reliable information, confidential support, financial independence, and practical freedom of action. The matrix is not a clinical diagnosis, a substitute for qualified help, or permission to investigate private people without lawful authority.

Handler and proxy analysis

Map roles without dehumanizing the people involved

“Puppet” is too broad for defensible analysis. Some actors are tasked and witting; others are fabricated identities, compromised channels, sincere believers, journalists, or ordinary people repeating what they think is true.

Hypothesis onlyOrchestrator / handlerPlans, resources, tasks, or synchronizes—if evidence supports that conclusion.
A

Witting proxy

Understands the objective and knowingly carries out a role.

Proof needed: authentic tasking, coordination, payment, access, or admission.
B

Covert persona

A fabricated identity operated to appear independent or local.

Proof needed: account control, creation history, shared infrastructure, or authenticated operator records.
C

Compromised channel

A legitimate account, outlet, or organization used without proper authorization.

Proof needed: access logs, owner confirmation, incident records, and content timeline.
D

Unwitting amplifier

Repeats a claim without knowing an originator's objective.

Analytic caution: reach does not equal complicity.
E

Trusted messenger

A real community or professional voice whose credibility affects interpretation.

Analytic caution: agreement may be independent and sincere.
F

Audience / decision maker

Receives competing information and retains the ability to accept, reject, delay, or investigate.

Protection goal: informed agency, not counter-manipulation.
01 · Origin

Where is the first known version?

Preserve the earliest available artifact, timestamp, URL, account, media file, and archive history. “First seen” is not automatically “first created.”

02 · Control

Who controls the relevant channel?

Use lawful account, domain, organizational, or platform records. Similar usernames or opinions are not proof of common control.

03 · Tasking

Is there evidence of direction?

Look for authenticated instructions, shared nonpublic materials, administration, payment, scheduling, or admissions. Infer tasking cautiously.

04 · Independence

How many origins are actually independent?

Build a provenance tree. Ten reposts, translations, or articles may still descend from one source.

05 · Effect

What behavior changed?

Measure a relevant decision, action, delay, purchase, movement, or trust indicator. Views and reactions alone are not the final effect.

06 · Alternatives

What else could explain the pattern?

Test organic concern, common-source copying, algorithmic clustering, satire, stale information, legitimate advocacy, coincidence, and ordinary error.

Fictional case · Operation Mirror Current

A resident encounters the same claim from six directions

A fictional claim says a municipal water-quality report is being concealed. A resident sees it in a family chat, a fake utility screenshot, a local blog, a recommendation feed, a neighborhood group, and photographs of empty bottled-water shelves.

Personal

A relative forwards a warning received from an unnamed coworker.

Unknown whether the relative is independent or repeating the same screenshot.
Institutional

An image imitates the utility's notice format but lacks a publication URL and date.

Could be fabricated, altered, or an old notice reused out of context.
News

A local blog cites “community reports” but provides no direct laboratory result.

The blog may be reporting the same family of posts rather than independent evidence.
Algorithmic

Search and recommendations display several high-engagement posts about the claim.

Personalization and popularity can create repeated exposure without one handler.
Proxy/community

A new neighborhood account urges residents to reject official testing.

Account age is a lead, not proof of a covert operator.
Environment

Photos show empty bottled-water shelves at one store.

Demand response may be an effect of the rumor, not evidence that the claim is true.
Initial defensive assessment · low confidenceThe claim is reaching the resident through six directions, creating high exposure convergence. The directions are not yet proven independent or centrally coordinated. Verify the current laboratory result and notice history, trace the screenshot, compare posting lineage, and avoid treating empty shelves as proof of contamination.

Resilience and response

Restore informed choice without building a counter-manipulation campaign

The response should protect the immediate decision, reduce the information void, and preserve evidence. It should not invent a covert counter-narrative or publicly accuse people without substantiation.

01

Verify the practical fact

Confirm what a person needs to decide now—status, location, time, eligibility, safety condition, or service availability.

02

Publish provenance

Provide the issuing authority, source document, measurement method, effective time, version, and next update.

03

Use channel diversity

Place the same factual correction in official, community, direct, accessible, and offline channels without fabricating independence.

04

Work through trusted messengers

Give genuine community and professional voices verifiable source material; do not script them as hidden proxies.

05

Add decision friction

For high-consequence claims, encourage a pause, direct-source check, second channel, and current timestamp before acting or sharing.

06

Preserve evidence lawfully

Retain original artifacts, hashes, timestamps, lineage, and public context while minimizing personal data and respecting access rules.

07

Measure behavior

Track whether decisions, movement, service use, reporting, or trust changed—not merely views, likes, and reposts.

08

Reassess and learn

Record what changed, which indicators were diagnostic, which assumptions failed, and when attribution confidence should rise or fall.

Mandatory legal and ethical gates

  • No targeting of real private individuals in this portfolio exercise.
  • No covert impersonation, harassment, doxxing, coercion, blackmail, or exploitation of medical or psychological vulnerabilities.
  • No collection beyond public, licensed, supplied, or properly authorized information.
  • No attribution of a handler, sponsor, or proxy relationship without independent evidence and qualified review.
  • No claim that message exposure proves belief, belief proves behavior, or behavior proves who caused it.
  • Real military or government influence activity requires specific lawful authority, approval, oversight, and mission scope; this page grants none.
