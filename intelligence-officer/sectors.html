Group 01 · Essential systems

Movement, power, communications, water, and shelter

01Logistics & transportPorts · rail · trucking · aviation · warehousing · last mile
How the system works
Suppliers book capacity; carriers move freight through ports, terminals, warehouses, and final delivery. Schedules, equipment, labor, fuel, customs, and information systems govern flow.
Questions to ask
Which commodity, route, handoff, carrier, and time window are affected? Is the problem capacity, access, labor, equipment, paperwork, weather, or demand? How much inventory is already downstream?
Investigation path
Build a shipment timeline from origin to receipt. Compare planned and actual arrival, dwell, pickup, and delivery times. Test port, warehouse, inland route, and demand explanations separately.
Issues and indicators
Rising dwell time, missed appointments, equipment imbalance, route closure, fuel constraint, labor action, customs hold, cyber outage, theft, congestion displacement, and false shortage narratives.
Evidence and specialists
Port and carrier notices, bills of lading where authorized, route telemetry, warehouse records, weather, customs data, inventory cover, dispatchers, terminal operators, drivers, and supply-chain planners.
02Energy & utilitiesGeneration · fuel · transmission · distribution · restoration
How the system works
Fuel or renewable inputs feed generation; transmission moves bulk power; distribution serves customers. Grid operators balance supply and demand while crews maintain and restore assets.
Questions to ask
Is the event generation, transmission, distribution, fuel, market, or control-system related? What load is affected? Are critical facilities islanded or backed up? What is the restoration sequence?
Investigation path
Compare outage geography and time with weather, equipment alarms, fuel status, maintenance, protection events, and demand. Distinguish loss of supply from local distribution damage and communications failure.
Issues and indicators
Frequency or voltage deviation, reserve shortage, transformer or substation failure, fuel interruption, wildfire shutoff, cyber incident, physical sabotage, restoration estimate drift, and cascading dependency failures.
Evidence and specialists
Utility outage maps, regulator filings, system-operator reports, authorized SCADA summaries, fuel inventories, weather, field assessments, grid operators, line crews, protection engineers, and emergency managers.
03TelecommunicationsFiber · mobile · satellite · spectrum · interconnection
How the system works
Access networks connect users to transport and core networks; routing, switching, radio spectrum, data centers, power, and interconnection deliver voice and data services.
Questions to ask
Which service, carrier, geography, and network layer are affected? Is failure in power, access, backhaul, routing, DNS, cloud dependency, congestion, or customer equipment? Are emergency calls affected?
Investigation path
Map the service path, establish a normal availability baseline, compare independent carriers and technologies, correlate alarms and route changes, and test whether application failure is being mistaken for network failure.
Issues and indicators
Fiber cut, tower power loss, backhaul saturation, route leak, DNS failure, signaling abuse, cloud-region dependency, jamming, equipment supply delay, misinformation about coverage, and prolonged restoration.
Evidence and specialists
Carrier status notices, authorized network telemetry, BGP and DNS observations, spectrum reports, outage crowdsourcing with bias controls, NOC staff, field technicians, RF engineers, and emergency communications coordinators.
04Water & wastewaterSource · treatment · pumping · distribution · collection
How the system works
Water is captured, treated, pumped, stored, and distributed; wastewater is collected, treated, and discharged. Power, chemicals, laboratories, operators, and control systems are critical inputs.
Questions to ask
Is the concern quantity, pressure, quality, treatment, distribution, or wastewater capacity? Which service zone and customers are affected? What tests confirm safety? How long can storage and backup power sustain operations?
Investigation path
Trace the affected zone from source through treatment and distribution. Compare laboratory results, pressure, flow, pump status, power, chemical inventory, breaks, and customer reports against baseline.
Issues and indicators
Source contamination, main break, low pressure, treatment excursion, chemical shortage, pump failure, cyber or sensor anomaly, flood inflow, drought, boil notice, and false public-health claims.
Evidence and specialists
Official advisories, water-quality reports, authorized plant records, pressure and flow summaries, weather and watershed data, utility operators, laboratory staff, civil engineers, public-health officials, and emergency managers.
05Housing, construction & land useLand · approvals · finance · labor · materials · inspection
How the system works
Housing depends on land, zoning, design, financing, utilities, labor, materials, permitting, construction, inspection, insurance, and occupancy.
Questions to ask
Is pressure caused by supply, affordability, displacement, damage, permitting, labor, materials, utilities, or financing? Which population and housing type are affected? What is the realistic construction or repair timeline?
Investigation path
Separate available units from affordable and habitable units. Compare permits, starts, completions, vacancies, rents, damage, insurance claims, utility connections, material lead times, and workforce capacity.
Issues and indicators
Rapid rent increase, permit backlog, vacancy mismatch, code failure, material shortage, contractor fraud, insurance retreat, disaster displacement, utility constraint, unsafe occupancy, and misleading headline averages.
Evidence and specialists
Planning and permit records, assessor and census data, housing inventories, court and insurance records where lawful, builders, inspectors, planners, utility engineers, housing providers, tenant advocates, and lenders.

Group 02 · Human systems

Food, health, education, population, culture, and influence

06Food & agricultureInputs · farms · processing · cold chain · retail
How the system works
Land, seed, feed, fertilizer, water, labor, machinery, processing, inspection, storage, transport, wholesale, and retail turn production into available food.
Questions to ask
Which product, region, season, and stage are affected? Is the issue production, processing, safety, transport, price, access, or demand? How many days of usable inventory remain?
Investigation path
Follow the product from inputs to shelf. Compare crop or livestock conditions, processing utilization, recall scope, cold-chain records, shipments, prices, sales, and representative inventories.
Issues and indicators
Weather loss, disease, input shortage, processing shutdown, contamination, cold-chain break, labor gap, transport delay, price spike, localized access failure, panic buying, and viral shortage imagery without context.
Evidence and specialists
Agricultural and food-safety reports, weather, commodity data, authorized temperature and inventory records, farmers, processors, veterinarians, food-safety specialists, distributors, grocers, and community food programs.
07Health & public healthPrevention · diagnosis · care · medicines · surveillance
How the system works
Public health monitors populations while clinics, laboratories, hospitals, pharmacies, emergency services, insurers, suppliers, and manufacturers deliver care.
Questions to ask
Is the signal a true change in illness or a change in testing, access, reporting, coding, or awareness? Which patients, facilities, medicines, staff, beds, and supplies are affected? What action is time-sensitive?
Investigation path
Define the case and denominator. Compare symptoms, tests, admissions, severity, geography, capacity, staffing, pharmacy supply, and reporting practices. Separate clinical evidence from public claims.
Issues and indicators
Cluster growth, excess demand, test positivity, severe outcomes, staff absence, drug or oxygen shortage, ambulance delay, cyber outage, contaminated product, health misinformation, privacy breach, and inequitable access.
Evidence and specialists
Official surveillance, laboratory and capacity summaries, recall notices, peer-reviewed research, authorized clinical data, epidemiologists, clinicians, pharmacists, laboratory scientists, supply managers, and community-health workers.
08Education & workforceSchools · training · credentials · labor pipelines
How the system works
Families, schools, colleges, employers, credentialing bodies, apprenticeships, and public programs develop knowledge and move people into occupations.
Questions to ask
Which skill is missing, at what proficiency, in which geography, and for how long? Is the constraint training capacity, completion, licensing, wages, retention, location, childcare, or hiring practice?
Investigation path
Map the pipeline from enrollment to qualified placement. Compare job demand, vacancies, wages, completion, certification, time-to-competence, attrition, migration, and employer requirements.
Issues and indicators
Teacher or specialist shortage, enrollment decline, credential bottleneck, skills mismatch, high turnover, inaccessible training, automation pressure, disinformation about institutions, cyber disruption, and unequal outcomes.
Evidence and specialists
Education and labor statistics, job postings with duplicate controls, licensing records, program outcomes, surveys, educators, workforce boards, employers, unions, credentialing bodies, students, and community organizations.
09Demographics & migrationPopulation · age · households · movement · services
How the system works
Births, deaths, household formation, aging, domestic movement, international migration, and displacement change the size and needs of communities.
Questions to ask
Which population, geography, and period are being measured? Is change permanent, seasonal, voluntary, economic, conflict-driven, or disaster-related? Which services gain or lose demand?
Investigation path
Compare censuses, estimates, school enrollment, housing, labor participation, service use, mobility indicators, and local accounts. Reconcile different definitions and reporting lags.
Issues and indicators
Aging workforce, sudden displacement, service mismatch, housing pressure, population loss, labor gaps, undocumented or double-counted movement, politicized statistics, stereotyping, and privacy risks.
Evidence and specialists
Official population data, enrollment, housing and labor records, surveys, humanitarian reporting, demographers, planners, service providers, cultural mediators, economists, and local organizations.
10Culture & languageMeaning · norms · identity · translation · trusted messengers
How the system works
Language, history, institutions, religion, media, family structures, and community networks shape how people interpret information and choose whom to trust.
Questions to ask
What does the message mean in local context? Which translation or symbol changes its meaning? Who is trusted, excluded, or at risk? Are observed behaviors being misread through an outside assumption?
Investigation path
Compare original-language content, translations, historical context, audience interpretation, messenger credibility, and behavior. Seek more than one culturally grounded perspective.
Issues and indicators
Mistranslation, stereotype, taboo, rumor, grievance activation, identity threat, diaspora differences, false consensus, manipulated imagery, outsider bias, and loss of context through reposting.
Evidence and specialists
Original-language material, local media, historical records, interviews with consent, linguists, anthropologists, historians, translators, community leaders, educators, and regional specialists.
11People, organizations & influencePsychology · leadership · incentives · narratives · behavior
How the system works
Individuals and organizations respond to incentives, identity, authority, risk, habit, social proof, information access, and institutional rules.
Questions to ask
What behavior is sought? Which audience and vulnerability are relevant? Is the content false, true but misframed, stale, satirical, mistaken, or deliberately deceptive? What observable effect occurred?
Investigation path
Use the defensive PSYOP matrix: claim, audience, channel, provenance, timing, amplification, behavior, effects, alternatives, and response. Test organic error, common-source copying, and coordination separately.
Issues and indicators
Emotional urgency, impersonation, selective truth, coordinated amplification, harassment, insider grievance, fabricated authority, manipulated media, information void, attribution pressure, and overreaction that amplifies the claim.
Evidence and specialists
Original content and metadata where lawful, archive history, platform transparency, official statements, audience research, communications staff, behavioral scientists, cultural specialists, investigators, and legal or privacy advisors.

Group 03 · Economic, industrial, and technical systems

Money, production, science, technology, and engineering

12Economics & financeOutput · prices · labor · credit · trade · public budgets
How the system works
Households, firms, governments, and markets allocate labor, capital, goods, and risk through prices, contracts, taxes, spending, trade, and credit.
Questions to ask
Is the signal nominal or inflation-adjusted, total or per person, national or local, temporary or structural? Who gains, who pays, and what behavior changes? Which policy or market mechanism transmits the effect?
Investigation path
Define the indicator and baseline. Compare output, employment, wages, prices, inventories, credit, trade, expectations, and sector-specific data. Test supply, demand, policy, and measurement explanations.
Issues and indicators
Inflation, recession, unemployment, debt stress, credit tightening, fiscal gap, trade shock, concentration, inequality, market manipulation, revised statistics, and headlines that confuse correlation with cause.
Evidence and specialists
Official economic series, budgets, audits, corporate filings, trade data, market prices, household surveys, economists, accountants, industry analysts, labor specialists, procurement staff, and local businesses.
13Banking & paymentsDeposits · credit · settlement · cards · cash · fraud controls
How the system works
Banks, payment processors, card networks, clearing systems, central banks, merchants, and cash logistics move and settle value while managing liquidity, credit, identity, and fraud.
Questions to ask
Is the failure authorization, clearing, settlement, liquidity, access, fraud control, merchant system, telecommunications, or customer device? Which payment rails and users are affected? Are balances safe but temporarily unavailable?
Investigation path
Map the transaction path and timestamps. Compare multiple rails, institutions, merchants, regions, and customer reports. Separate a service outage from fraud, insolvency, access denial, and misinformation.
Issues and indicators
Payment decline spike, settlement delay, cash shortage, bank run rumor, credential theft, fraud surge, sanctions exposure, liquidity pressure, processor outage, telecom dependency, and misleading screenshots.
Evidence and specialists
Official service notices, regulator reports, authorized transaction summaries, fraud patterns, merchant data, treasury staff, payment operators, bank risk teams, economists, cybersecurity teams, and consumer-protection officials.
14Manufacturing & mineralsMaterials · components · tooling · production · quality
How the system works
Mines, refiners, suppliers, factories, maintenance teams, quality systems, logistics, and customers transform raw materials into components and finished goods.
Questions to ask
Which material, part, process, tool, plant, and customer are affected? Is the constraint capacity, yield, maintenance, energy, labor, quality, transport, export control, or single-source dependency? What substitutes are qualified?
Investigation path
Build the bill-of-material and process chain. Compare capacity, utilization, yield, scrap, downtime, lead times, inventories, quality escapes, supplier concentration, and substitution time.
Issues and indicators
Mine or plant outage, low yield, tooling failure, counterfeit part, quality recall, energy shortage, export restriction, supplier insolvency, cyber outage, maintenance backlog, and hidden tier-two dependency.
Evidence and specialists
Production and trade data, corporate filings, procurement records, patents, standards, recall notices, authorized quality records, plant engineers, metallurgists, maintenance, sourcing, quality, and logistics specialists.
15Science & researchHypothesis · method · measurement · replication · review
How the system works
Researchers define questions, select methods, collect and analyze data, publish findings, face peer review, and build confidence through replication and converging evidence.
Questions to ask
What exactly was measured? What is the comparison group, sample, uncertainty, effect size, and limitation? Was the result replicated? Does the paper support the public claim being made from it?
Investigation path
Read the method and data before the headline. Check preregistration, sample selection, controls, statistics, conflicts, peer review, correction history, related literature, and independent replication.
Issues and indicators
Small or biased sample, p-hacking, confounding, model overreach, nonreplication, publication bias, withdrawn paper, preprint treated as consensus, press-release exaggeration, and fabricated or misused evidence.
Evidence and specialists
Primary papers, data and code where available, protocols, corrections, systematic reviews, standards, domain scientists, statisticians, methodologists, laboratory staff, research-integrity officers, and librarians.
16Technology & cybersecuritySoftware · cloud · identity · networks · data · operations
How the system works
Developers, vendors, cloud and network operators, identity systems, endpoints, data platforms, support teams, and security operations deliver and protect digital services.
Questions to ask
Which service, tenant, user, host, dependency, and time window are affected? Is the event malicious, accidental, misconfigured, capacity-related, or third-party? What telemetry should exist if each explanation is true?
Investigation path
Preserve time and scope; establish a normal baseline; correlate identity, endpoint, network, cloud, application, and change records; test competing hypotheses; assess exposure, effect, persistence, and recovery.
Issues and indicators
Credential abuse, vulnerability exploitation, phishing, data exposure, malware, denial of service, supply-chain compromise, misconfiguration, telemetry gap, parser failure, unauthorized automation, and premature actor attribution.
Evidence and specialists
Authorized logs and telemetry, vendor advisories, CVEs, configurations, change records, packet or endpoint evidence, developers, administrators, SOC analysts, incident responders, cloud and identity engineers, legal, and privacy teams.

17Engineering & constructionRequirements · design · build · test · operate
How the system works
Civil, mechanical, electrical, structural, industrial, and systems engineers translate requirements into designs, materials, construction, verification, maintenance, and safe operation.
Questions to ask
What requirement, load, tolerance, interface, environment, code, and safety factor apply? Is the observation a design defect, construction error, material failure, maintenance issue, overload, or sensor problem?
Investigation path
Compare as-designed, as-built, as-operated, and as-observed states. Review drawings, changes, materials, inspections, tests, loads, maintenance, environment, failure sequence, and similar assets.
Issues and indicators
Crack or deformation growth, overheating, vibration, corrosion, water intrusion, tolerance stack-up, unapproved change, counterfeit material, inspection gap, deferred maintenance, and false certainty from a single image.
Evidence and specialists
Drawings, specifications, calculations, codes, inspection and test records, sensor history, maintenance, photographs with scale and time, engineers of record, trades, inspectors, operators, materials laboratories, and safety staff.

Group 04 · Governance, security, and strategic systems

Authority, defense, diplomacy, emergencies, and environment

18Government & lawAuthority · policy · budget · administration · review
How the system works
Constitutions, laws, regulations, budgets, elected bodies, agencies, courts, local governments, contractors, auditors, and the public shape authority and delivery.
Questions to ask
Which jurisdiction and institution has legal authority, funding, delivery responsibility, and review power? Is a statement a proposal, enacted law, funded program, regulation, court ruling, or operational practice?
Investigation path
Trace authority → appropriation → rule → implementation → contract → service → oversight. Compare the official text, effective date, budget, agency practice, local variation, and appeal process.
Issues and indicators
Jurisdiction gap, unfunded mandate, procurement delay, administrative capacity, policy contradiction, corruption risk, court challenge, outdated guidance, emergency power, politicized claim, and confusion between announced and implemented action.
Evidence and specialists
Official laws, rules, budgets, meeting records, procurement, audits, court records, agency data, public administrators, legislative staff, lawyers, auditors, program managers, contractors, and affected communities.
19Defense & military systemsMission · readiness · command · sustainment · protection
How the system works
Political direction, command, personnel, training, intelligence, logistics, maintenance, communications, bases, industry, allies, and doctrine generate and sustain capability.
Questions to ask
What mission and time horizon are being assessed? Is the evidence about capability, readiness, posture, signaling, or intent? Which logistics, maintenance, training, command, legal, and alliance constraints apply?
Investigation path
Separate observed assets and activity from inferred readiness and intent. Compare baselines, exercise patterns, logistics, maintenance, mobilization, command communications, official statements, and plausible deception.
Issues and indicators
Readiness gap, supply constraint, maintenance backlog, mobilization signal, communications disruption, exercise misread as operation, concealment, strategic messaging, alliance friction, civilian impact, escalation, and attribution uncertainty.
Evidence and specialists
Official releases and budgets, procurement, imagery and geospatial data where lawful, logistics and trade data, doctrine, specialist research, regional experts, logisticians, engineers, historians, legal advisers, and alliance analysts.
20Security & emergency responsePrevention · preparedness · response · recovery · learning
How the system works
Emergency management, fire, law enforcement, medical services, public works, utilities, communications, public information, nongovernmental partners, and businesses coordinate through plans and incident command.
Questions to ask
What happened, where, when, who is at risk, and who has command? Which life-safety actions are urgent? What capabilities, routes, communications, shelters, hospitals, and supplies remain available?
Investigation path
Establish an incident timeline and common operating picture. Validate reports by source and location; map hazards, resources, dependencies, decisions, unmet needs, secondary effects, and recovery criteria.
Issues and indicators
Conflicting reports, communications loss, responder overload, hospital saturation, evacuation bottleneck, hazardous material, infrastructure cascade, rumor, fraud, volunteer convergence, evidence loss, and inequitable recovery.
Evidence and specialists
Official alerts, dispatch and incident records where authorized, weather, maps, utility status, field reports, emergency managers, first responders, public-health staff, engineers, public-information officers, community groups, and recovery planners.
21International relationsDiplomacy · alliances · trade · sanctions · international institutions
How the system works
States, international institutions, alliances, firms, treaties, trade, finance, energy, law, domestic politics, and public opinion shape cross-border cooperation and competition.
Questions to ask
Which actors, interests, obligations, dependencies, and domestic constraints shape behavior? Is an action symbolic, diplomatic, economic, legal, coercive, cooperative, or operational? Which partner or population absorbs the effect?
Investigation path
Build actor, interest, dependency, and timeline maps. Compare official positions with implementation, trade and finance flows, alliance behavior, domestic politics, historical patterns, and plausible alternative outcomes.
Issues and indicators
Diplomatic rupture, sanctions, export control, treaty breach, alliance strain, coercive trade, resource competition, migration pressure, strategic signaling, proxy behavior, escalation, and public statements that exceed observed action.
Evidence and specialists
Treaties, official statements, votes, budgets, trade and finance data, geospatial reporting where lawful, diplomats, regional experts, international lawyers, economists, historians, humanitarian organizations, and local specialists.
22Environment & climate systemsAir · water · soil · ecosystems · hazards · adaptation
How the system works
Atmosphere, watersheds, oceans, soils, ecosystems, land use, industry, infrastructure, and human communities interact across local and global time scales.
Questions to ask
What hazard, exposure, vulnerability, geography, and time horizon are being assessed? Is the signal weather, climate trend, pollution, land-use change, ecological loss, measurement change, or a combination? Who bears the impact?
Investigation path
Separate event attribution from impact assessment. Compare observations, historical baselines, models, land and water use, emissions or discharge, infrastructure exposure, health and economic effects, and adaptation capacity.
Issues and indicators
Drought, flood, wildfire, heat, erosion, contamination, habitat loss, crop stress, water conflict, infrastructure exposure, unequal impact, model misuse, cherry-picked dates, transboundary effects, and delayed recovery.
Evidence and specialists
Weather and climate records, environmental monitoring, satellite and land-use data, watershed and ecological reports, public-health and insurance data, climate scientists, ecologists, hydrologists, engineers, planners, economists, and local communities.

Cross-sector investigations

When one sector is never enough

Use these starting patterns to connect sector evidence without merging unlike measurements into a single unsupported conclusion.

01

Regional shortage claim

Trace production, logistics, inventory, prices, retail access, household purchasing, media claims, and government response. Distinguish physical shortage from distribution delay and affordability.

02

Infrastructure outage

Map power, telecommunications, cloud, water, transport, payment, emergency, and supplier dependencies. Build a restoration sequence and test common-cause failure.

03

Public-health cluster

Combine case definition, testing, care capacity, food or water exposure, workplace and school patterns, mobility, communications, and supply constraints.

04

Industrial production shock

Map minerals, energy, labor, tooling, quality, logistics, trade controls, finance, technology, customers, substitutions, and recovery lead time.

05

Influence campaign concern

Separate claim accuracy, provenance, audience, amplification, coordination, observable behavior, attribution, and practical response using the matrix.

06

Community displacement

Connect hazard, housing, utilities, health, transport, education, employment, payments, demographics, language, government authority, and recovery support.

Required output

For every investigation, write the customer and deadline, observed facts, key judgment, confidence, alternatives, collection gaps, affected sectors, next action, owner, and review trigger.
