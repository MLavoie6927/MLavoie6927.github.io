01 / Levels of thought

Tactics are not strategy

A sound assessment identifies which level owns the question. A tactical success can undermine policy; an elegant strategy can fail when operations cannot supply it; an operational delay can look like hostile intent when it is simply capacity.

01 · Policy & governance

Why act, under whose authority?

Defines the lawful public objective, legitimacy, constraints, oversight, civilian obligations, and acceptable outcome.

02 / Strategic estimate

The nine-test decision matrix

This original matrix turns classical planning themes—purpose, environment, leadership, discipline, logistics, cost, adaptation, intelligence, and restraint—into a public-safe analytic checklist.

01Decision & clock

Who must decide what, by when?

Name the customer, choice, deadline, geography, time horizon, and what would change the decision.

02End state

What condition counts as success?

Describe a measurable outcome, the people affected, what must remain protected, and the acceptable stopping point.

03Authority & legitimacy

May the organization act?

Identify jurisdiction, mandate, consent, oversight, legal review, public obligations, and prohibited actions.

04Environment & timing

What does context permit?

Map terrain, weather, geography, calendars, politics, culture, infrastructure, baseline, and timing windows.

05Leadership & organization

Can people coordinate?

Test decision rights, competence, incentives, training, communications, trust, discipline, and handoffs.

06Means & sustainment

Can the effort be supplied?

Quantify money, people, equipment, suppliers, transport, maintenance, information, and replacement time.

07Alternatives & adaptation

What else explains the picture?

Compare direct and indirect options, second-order effects, adversary or stakeholder reactions, and reversible moves.

08Intelligence & warning

What must be known next?

State assumptions, gaps, best source stack, deception risk, indicators, collection owner, and confidence.

09Cost, off-ramp & review

When should the plan change?

Set resource ceilings, harm thresholds, escalation controls, review dates, termination criteria, and recovery duties.

Statecraft investigation questions

04 / Human relationships

Trust is an intelligence capability—and an obligation

Useful human-source work starts with lawful purpose, genuine curiosity, listening, and long-term stewardship. The analyst seeks understanding without pretending friendship, exploiting vulnerability, or turning a person into a disposable transaction.

01

Purpose

Define the legitimate question, authority, necessity, and limits before approaching anyone.

02

Placement

Ask what the person could know firsthand and what lies outside their access or competence.

03

Respectful approach

Be honest about role and boundaries where required; protect consent, safety, privacy, and refusal.

04

Listen & clarify

Use open questions, neutral follow-ups, chronology, concrete examples, and the source's own uncertainty.

05

Validate

Separate direct observation from inference, test memory and motive, and seek independent records or perspectives.

06

Stewardship

Minimize exposure, honor handling promises, avoid unnecessary secrecy, record caveats, and close the loop responsibly.

Ethical influence

Work with people willingly

  • Shared purpose and informed choice
  • Truthful constraints and realistic expectations
  • Freedom to disagree, stop, or seek review
  • Long-term trust matters more than a one-time outcome
Manipulation warning

Stop when autonomy is being bypassed

  • Deception used for advantage rather than protection
  • Pressure, dependency, humiliation, or manufactured urgency
  • Concealed conflicts, retaliation, or exploitation of distress
  • Success measured only by compliance
Strategic composure

Step off the emotional battlefield

  • Pause and describe observable facts
  • Name the emotion without making it evidence
  • Ask what data would change the first reaction
  • Re-enter with a bounded, reversible next step
Questions that improve information quality
“What did you directly see, hear, measure, or do?”“What happened immediately before and after?”“Who else was positioned to know independently?”“Which part are you least certain about?”“What record could confirm or contradict this?”“What would make your explanation wrong?”

05 / Defensive recognition

Eight patterns to recognize without overclaiming

These patterns are presented for analysis, resilience, and protective decisions—not as instructions for conducting violence. Similar shapes appear in cyber incidents, markets, organizations, disasters, and influence activity, but pattern resemblance alone does not prove intent.

01 · Feigned withdrawal

An apparent retreat may be a lure

Observe: sudden disengagement paired with preserved capability or prepared follow-on activity.

Alternative: genuine de-escalation, exhaustion, resource loss, or reporting delay.

Protect: verify independently; avoid irreversible pursuit; define a hold point.

02 · Envelopment / pincer

Pressure arrives from multiple directions

Observe: a central issue fixes attention while separate channels constrain options or communications.

Alternative: independent failures, routine congestion, or common-cause disruption.

Protect: map dependencies, preserve alternate routes, and test coordination.

03 · Ambush / lure

A trigger draws action into prepared risk

Observe: unusually attractive, urgent, or provocative information followed by a predictable exposure.

Alternative: coincidence, poor design, a legitimate opportunity, or misunderstanding.

Protect: authenticate the trigger, slow the decision, and use controlled verification.

04 · Swarming / saturation

Many small demands overwhelm attention

Observe: simultaneous low-level events consume triage, communications, or service capacity.

Alternative: seasonal demand, cascade failure, organic attention, or duplicate reporting.

Protect: deduplicate, prioritize critical functions, and preserve reserve capacity.

05 · Defeat in detail

Disconnected elements fail one at a time

Observe: isolated teams face concentrated pressure without mutual support or shared visibility.

Alternative: local mismanagement, staffing gaps, or unrelated timing.

Protect: share warning, coordinate thresholds, and restore cross-team support.

06 · Interdiction

Upstream access is disrupted before the crisis point

Observe: transport, payments, communications, supplies, or permissions fail along a dependency path.

Alternative: maintenance, regulation, commercial dispute, weather, or capacity.

Protect: identify chokepoints, alternatives, stock cover, and restoration owners.

07 · Siege / isolation

Connection and replenishment steadily narrow

Observe: access, information, finance, movement, or support channels close over time.

Alternative: safety restrictions, infrastructure damage, compliance action, or market exit.

Protect: maintain lawful communication, welfare checks, alternate supply, and escalation paths.

08 · Defense in depth

Layered controls absorb failure

Observe: prevention, detection, containment, recovery, and oversight remain independent enough to limit impact.

Failure mode: layers share one dependency, credential, vendor, clock, or untested assumption.

Protect: test correlated failure, failover, restoration time, and decision authority.

07 / Finished intelligence product

The seven-line decision brief

A strategic assessment is useful only when a decision maker can see the judgment, evidence, uncertainty, and next move quickly. The standard is simple: Observed, assessed, unproven—never blended together.

  1. Decision: name the customer, choice, deadline, and threshold.
  2. Key judgment: give the answer first, with confidence and time horizon.
  3. Observed: list only facts directly supported by the evidence ledger.
  4. Assessed: explain the inference and why it fits better than alternatives.
  5. Unproven: name attractive claims that the evidence does not establish.
  6. Action: propose an owner, a reversible step, and a protection measure.
  7. Review: state the gap, collection task, indicator, and time that reopens the judgment.
Quality test

Can another analyst reproduce the reasoning?

The brief should link each important claim to a dated source record, preserve disagreement, explain confidence, identify legal or ethical gates, and show exactly what observation would change the recommendation.
