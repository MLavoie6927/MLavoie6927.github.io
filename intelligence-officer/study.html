Scenario practice

Choose first. Reveal second.

Each item is an original practice prompt with a best answer, short rationale, and memory cue. These are educational examples, not official exam questions.

Module 01

Decision framing and sector literacy

Turn a vague request into a time-bound question and find the critical handoff.

Question 01 · Decision

A city manager asks, “Is our food supply in trouble?” A road closure is reported, and a vendor call is scheduled in two hours. What should the analyst ask first?

  1. Which actor caused the closure?
  2. Which decision the manager must make, by when, for which locations and products.
  3. Whether all local stores have empty shelves.
  4. How many articles mention the closure.
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. The customer, choice, deadline, geography, and product scope define useful collection. Actor attribution and article counts do not yet answer the decision.

Memory cue: frame the decision before collecting more signals.

Question 02 · Dependencies

A hospital reports delayed medicine deliveries. Which cross-sector map is most useful to build first?

  1. Medicine manufacturer → distributor → cold chain → road or air route → hospital inventory.
  2. Only the hospital's website traffic.
  3. Only national medicine sales.
  4. A list of every hospital employee.
Reveal answer and reasoning

A. The delivery chain identifies measurable handoffs and possible bottlenecks. A specialist must validate storage and clinical substitution constraints.

Memory cue: inputs, handoffs, constraints, output.

Question 03 · Specialist depth

A port notice shows a backlog. Which question to a logistics operator is most diagnostic?

  1. “Do you agree there is a crisis?”
  2. “What timestamp separates unloading delay from inland pickup delay for the affected shipments?”
  3. “Who is to blame?”
  4. “Can you forecast the whole economy?”
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. It asks for a measurable distinction between two explanations and respects the operator's actual area of access.

Memory cue: ask specialists for the handoff they can directly observe.

Module 02

Sources and independence

Thirty source types create options; each individual item still needs validation.

Question 04 · Corroboration

Three news articles cite the same anonymous social post about a power outage. How many independent origins are established?

  1. Three.
  2. Four.
  3. One, pending verification of the original post.
  4. Zero articles.
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Repetition is not independent corroboration. Trace the earliest source and compare with an official utility report or authorized measurement.

Memory cue: count origins, not copies.

Question 05 · Handling

An analyst finds a private hospital dashboard through an exposed link. What is the sound next step?

  1. Copy all patient data before access closes.
  2. Use it because the URL loads.
  3. Avoid accessing or distributing restricted records and seek authorized, minimal data through the proper owner.
  4. Post screenshots to obtain public validation.
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Technical reachability is not authorization. Privacy and access controls remain relevant to collection.

Memory cue: public link does not equal lawful access.

Module 03

Alternatives, uncertainty, and counterintelligence

Test explanations against disconfirming evidence and distinguish capability from intent.

Question 06 · Competing hypotheses

A store photo appears to show a shortage. Which observation would best challenge the “regional shortage” hypothesis?

  1. More reposts of the photo.
  2. Representative inventory and sales records showing normal stock in the affected region.
  3. A strong emotional reaction online.
  4. A manager's unsupported prediction.
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Representative stock and sales records test the regional claim. A selected image has no reliable denominator.

Memory cue: seek the evidence that could change your mind.

Question 07 · Confidence

Two apparently separate distributor reports may share one carrier. You have no shipment logs. How should confidence in the precise duration of delay be described?

  1. High; there are two reports.
  2. Low, with the shared-carrier and missing-log limitations explained.
  3. Certain, because weather was bad.
  4. Do not state uncertainty.
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Confidence concerns the evidentiary basis for the judgment. It is distinct from how consequential the outcome would be.

Memory cue: importance does not raise confidence.

Question 08 · Attribution

Several accounts repeat a false evacuation claim. What can you conclude from repetition alone?

  1. A named foreign actor is responsible.
  2. The posts are synchronized by one operator.
  3. The claim is circulating; origin and coordination still require evidence.
  4. The evacuation is real.
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. Similar wording can arise from copying, common sources, automation, or coordination. Preserve these alternatives before attributing intent or actor.

Memory cue: content similarity is a lead, not attribution.

Module 04

Influence activity and PSYOP analysis

Analyze the information environment defensively using the matrix below.

Question 09 · Influence

A post urges residents to abandon a shelter after a storm. What is the safest first analytical move?

  1. Share the post to warn everyone.
  2. Verify the claim and timestamp against the responsible emergency authority; preserve the original and its provenance.
  3. Label a named actor immediately.
  4. Assume the account's audience is its operator.
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Protect the public decision first while retaining evidence. The content may be mistaken, manipulated, or official but stale.

Memory cue: verify status before attributing intent.

Question 10 · Matrix interpretation

The same message appears on five accounts, all reposting one original. Which matrix field prevents a false “five-source” claim?

  1. Audience only.
  2. Provenance and independence.
  3. Color palette.
  4. Post length.
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. Source lineage shows five copies of one origin. Distribution breadth is different from evidentiary independence.

Memory cue: reach is not reliability.

Module 05

Briefing and feedback

Give the customer a decision-ready statement and a way to update it.

Question 11 · Briefing

A procurement lead has ten minutes. Which lead sentence is strongest?

  1. “Many interesting posts have appeared.”
  2. “A local delay is plausible, but its cause and duration are unconfirmed; reserve a reversible alternate route pending shipment data.”
  3. “The whole region will run out of food.”
  4. “Please read all 30 source categories.”
Reveal answer and reasoning

B. It states the judgment, uncertainty, and a proportionate decision option up front.

Memory cue: judgment → reason → action → review.

Question 12 · Feedback

New route logs show normal port transit but a warehouse bottleneck. What should the analyst do?

  1. Keep the original port assessment because it was published.
  2. Silently delete the original assessment.
  3. Update the judgment, explain the new evidence and changed reasoning, and check whether the prior recommendation still fits.
  4. Ignore logs until an expert confirms the old hypothesis.
Reveal answer and reasoning

C. A useful intelligence product records what changed and why; it does not defend an earlier call for its own sake.

Memory cue: review against reality, not against pride.

The PSYOP analysis matrix

This is a defensive analysis method for examining a possible influence campaign, not a plan for conducting one. A viral claim alone does not establish psychological operations, coordination, or a particular actor. The analyst separates the message, its distribution, observable effects, and competing explanations.

Fictional case · Riverbend flood

Counterintelligence and ethics boundary

Do not use the matrix to target people, infer identity from weak traces, or convert suspicion into accusation. Preserve source context, minimize personal data, follow lawful access rules, and escalate public-safety corrections through the responsible authority.

Two-minute briefing drill

Practice the handoff

60 seconds · Say the judgment

“The shelter-closure screenshot conflicts with the current county page. We have one unverified original and several copies; origin and intent are unknown.”

40 seconds · Explain the test

“We are checking the first upload against archived notices and asking the emergency authority to confirm its latest status and timestamp.”

20 seconds · State action and trigger

“Point residents to the official notice. Rebrief immediately if the authority changes status or we verify a manipulated original.”

Before publishing a real briefRecord the actual decision maker, deadline, provenance, lawful handling, contrary evidence, confidence, and review owner. Never substitute this fictional exercise for live verification.
