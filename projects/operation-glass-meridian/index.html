    Strategic Warning and the Applied Intelligence Cycle

    Assessing an Emerging Hybrid Threat

    Priority Intelligence Question: What is the most likely strategic objective behind Norland's current military, cyber, information, and diplomatic activities toward Estavia during the next six months?

    Your assessment must distinguish between what is known, assessed, assumed, uncertain, and currently unknowable.

    0% complete Responses save automatically in this browser.
    01

    Executive Intelligence Assessment

    1. What is your principal intelligence judgment?
    2. What calibrated probability term best describes that judgment?
    3. What is your confidence level: low, moderate, or high?
    4. What are the two to four strongest supporting judgments?
    5. What is the strongest alternative explanation?
    6. What indicators would most likely change the assessment?
    7. Can a senior policymaker understand the assessment without reading the full paper?
    02

    Intelligence Question and Decision Context

    1. What decision do policymakers actually need to make?
    2. Why does this intelligence question matter now?
    3. What is the relevant six-month time horizon?
    4. What can intelligence reasonably determine?
    5. What can intelligence not determine with certainty?
    6. Are you answering the Priority Intelligence Question rather than a more convenient question?
    03

    Intelligence Environment

    1. Which political developments are directly relevant to Norland's strategic objective?
    2. Which military developments materially change Norland's operational capability?
    3. What cyber activity represents espionage, pre-positioning, coercion, or attack preparation?
    4. What economic or logistical changes support sustained operations?
    5. What geographic factors shape likely routes or limited territorial objectives?
    6. How is diplomacy being used alongside military pressure?
    7. How is the information environment being shaped for coercion, justification, or deception?
    8. What background information is interesting but not decision-relevant?
    04

    Collection and Source Evaluation

    1. Which HUMINT sources have access to preparations, and which have access to leadership intent?
    2. What does SIGINT establish about readiness, command relationships, or operational tempo?
    3. What does GEOINT/IMINT establish physically, and what can imagery not reveal?
    4. Which OSINT and SOCMINT reports are independently verified?
    5. What does cyber intelligence reveal about access, capability, and pre-positioning?
    6. What does diplomatic reporting reveal about coercion or ambiguity?
    7. What do FININT, economic, energy, transportation, MEDINT, and TECHINT contribute?
    8. How reliable is each important source?
    9. How credible is the specific information?
    10. Does the source have direct or indirect access?
    11. Is the report independently corroborated?
    12. Could apparent corroboration actually be circular reporting?
    13. How timely is the reporting?
    14. How vulnerable is it to deliberate deception?
    15. What major intelligence gaps remain?
    05

    Analysis of Competing Hypotheses

    H1 — Coercive Diplomacy: Norland seeks concessions through intimidation without intending major military action.
    H2 — Limited Military Operation: Norland is preparing a geographically restricted operation to seize territory, establish a buffer zone, or compel negotiations.
    H3 — Large-Scale Invasion: Norland is preparing for a sustained conventional invasion.
    H4 — Strategic Deception: Norland deliberately creates indicators of imminent attack while pursuing another objective.
    1. Which evidence is consistent with each hypothesis?
    2. Which evidence is inconsistent with each hypothesis?
    3. Which evidence is strongly diagnostic?
    4. Which evidence is weakly diagnostic or dual-use?
    5. Which expected indicators are missing despite reasonable collection coverage?
    6. Which reports may have been deliberately exposed or manipulated?
    7. What evidence would be difficult to explain if H1 were true?
    8. What evidence would be difficult to explain if H2 were true?
    9. What evidence would be difficult to explain if H3 were true?
    10. What evidence would be difficult to explain if H4 were true?
    11. Are you counting supporting reports instead of weighting inconsistencies?
    12. Is an additional H5 analytically justified?
    06

    Structured Analytic Techniques

    1. How does ACH change your initial intuition?
    2. What are the key assumptions behind your preferred hypothesis?
    3. Which assumptions are most vulnerable?
    4. What indicators would warn of escalation or de-escalation?
    5. What would a devil's advocate argue against your principal judgment?
    6. How would a red team attempt to manipulate Estavian analysts?
    7. If your assessment failed, what would a premortem identify as the likely analytic error?
    8. Which alternative futures belong in the cone of plausibility?
    9. What critical what-if event would most change the case?
    10. What does a timeline reveal that isolated reports do not?
    11. Which evidence fails a quality-of-information check?
    12. Which evidence is genuinely diagnostic?

    Requirement: ACH plus at least two additional structured analytic techniques.

    07

    Indicators and Warning Framework

    1. What would count as an early-warning indicator weeks or months before action?
    2. What would count as operational warning days or weeks before action?
    3. What would count only as confirmation that execution is imminent or underway?
    4. Would blood-bank mobilization materially increase concern?
    5. Would large ammunition distribution materially change H3?
    6. Would expanded field hospitals change the estimated scale of operations?
    7. What does bridging-unit positioning reveal when combined with geography?
    8. What would electronic-warfare deployment indicate?
    9. What would reserve activation indicate?
    10. What would civilian or diplomatic evacuation indicate?
    11. What would nationwide air-defense dispersal indicate?
    12. What would systematic military leave cancellation indicate?
    13. When does cyber access become cyber attack preparation?
    14. What transportation-control changes would indicate broader mobilization?
    15. What propaganda shifts would create political justification for force?
    16. What command-and-control changes would be difficult to fake?
    17. Why does confirmation without sufficient warning have limited intelligence value?
    08

    Cognitive Bias and Analytic Risk

    1. Where could confirmation bias cause analysts to overvalue invasion indicators?
    2. How could anchoring on the starting estimate distort later judgments?
    3. Where might mirror imaging distort interpretation of Norland's choices?
    4. How could vivid cyberattacks or viral imagery create availability bias?
    5. Could the newest report receive excessive weight because of recency bias?
    6. Where could groupthink emerge inside the fusion room?
    7. When might analysts prematurely close on H2 or H3?
    8. Could policymaker preferences politicize the assessment?
    9. Where is overconfidence most dangerous?
    10. How does deliberate deception exploit cognitive bias?
    11. Which structured techniques reduce these risks?
    12. Which risks remain even after structured analysis?
    09

    Intelligence Gaps and Collection Strategy

    1. What is the single most consequential intelligence gap?
    2. Do we know whether Norland's senior leadership authorized offensive operations?
    3. What remains unknown about ammunition distribution?
    4. What remains unknown about reserve activation?
    5. What remains unknown about wartime medical preparation?
    6. What remains unknown about aviation or maritime mobilization?
    7. What remains unknown about destructive cyber payload activation?
    8. What remains unknown about the intended territorial objective?
    9. Which intelligence discipline is best suited to each gap?
    10. What specific information requirement should be tasked?
    11. What result would increase confidence?
    12. What result would decrease confidence?
    13. Is the collection opportunity realistic?
    14. What collection risks exist?
    15. Could Norland manipulate the collection channel?
    16. Would answering the gap materially change a policymaker's decision?
    10

    Known, Assessed, Assumed, Uncertain, Unknowable

    1. Which facts are directly established by sufficiently reliable reporting?
    2. Which conclusions are analytic inferences?
    3. Which propositions are assumptions required by the analysis?
    4. Which questions remain uncertain because evidence is incomplete or contradictory?
    5. Which questions are currently unknowable with available collection?
    6. Have you written an inference as though it were a fact?
    7. Have you disguised an intelligence gap as a conclusion?
    11

    Capability Versus Intent

    1. What military operations can Norland physically conduct with the force observed?
    2. What can its current logistics sustain?
    3. What can its cyber access enable?
    4. Which capabilities are present but not activated?
    5. What evidence speaks directly to leadership intent?
    6. What evidence speaks only to capability?
    7. Are you converting readiness into evidence of a political decision?
    8. What collection would narrow the capability-intent gap?
    12

    Deception Analysis

    1. If Norland wanted Estavia to conclude invasion was imminent, what indicators would it intentionally expose?
    2. If Norland planned a real operation but wanted it dismissed as an exercise, what would it conceal?
    3. Which current indicators are cheap to fake?
    4. Which indicators are expensive to fake?
    5. Which indicators are operationally necessary?
    6. Which indicators are difficult to conceal?
    7. Does the planted invasion document invalidate the independently observed physical preparations?
    8. Can genuine military preparation and strategic deception occur simultaneously?
    9. What evidence would distinguish deception supporting coercion from deception masking a real operation?
    13

    Probability and Confidence

    1. What probability do you assign to H1?
    2. What probability do you assign to H2?
    3. What probability do you assign to H3?
    4. What probability do you assign to H4?
    5. Do the probabilities total 100%?
    6. Which verbal estimative term corresponds to each probability?
    7. What is your separate confidence judgment?
    8. Why are probability and confidence different variables?
    9. What evidence most limits confidence?
    14

    Falsification and Reconsideration

    1. What evidence would cause you to increase your principal assessment?
    2. What evidence would cause you to decrease it?
    3. What evidence would cause you to abandon your preferred hypothesis?
    4. What cluster of independent indicators would force rapid reassessment?
    5. What apparent change should be ignored unless behavior changes as well?
    6. Can your assessment actually be falsified?
    15

    Final Assessment

    1. What is the most likely outcome during the next six months?
    2. What is the strongest alternative outcome?
    3. What is the most dangerous plausible outcome?
    4. Why is the most dangerous scenario not necessarily the most likely?
    5. What is your confidence level?
    6. What are the principal warning indicators?
    7. What are the implications for senior decision-makers?
    8. Under what conditions should the judgment be reconsidered?
    16

    Three-Minute Executive Brief and Oral Defense

    Executive Brief
    1. What do we assess?
    2. Why?
    3. How confident are we?
    4. What is the strongest alternative?
    5. What do we not know?
    6. What would change the judgment?
    National Security Adviser Challenge Questions
    1. How do you know your sources are independent?
    2. Why is the field-hospital evidence not enough to conclude war is coming?
    3. What evidence contradicts your preferred hypothesis?
    4. Are you confusing capability with intent?
    5. Could Norland deliberately be feeding you these indicators?
    6. What is your most important intelligence gap?
    7. Which source would you discard first?
    8. What would prove you wrong?
    9. Why is the most dangerous scenario not the most likely?
    10. If you had one additional collection opportunity, what would you collect and why?
    17

    Graduate Paper Submission Checklist

    • 4,000–6,000 words
    • Graduate academic level
    • Minimum 12 scholarly or professional sources
    • APA 7th edition
    • Executive Intelligence Assessment no longer than 500 words
    • ACH plus at least two additional structured analytic techniques
    • Known / Assessed / Assumed / Uncertain / Unknowable explicitly distinguished
    • Calibrated estimative terminology used appropriately
    • Reliability, credibility, access, corroboration, timeliness, lineage, and deception risk evaluated
    • Indicators and warning framework included
    • Collection priorities tied to decision relevance
    • Conditions that would change or falsify the judgment stated explicitly
    The purpose is not to predict the future with certainty. The purpose is to produce a disciplined estimate of an uncertain future using incomplete, contradictory, and potentially deceptive information—and to explain precisely why that judgment deserves a policymaker's confidence.
