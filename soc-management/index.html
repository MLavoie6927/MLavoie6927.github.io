Enterprise operating model

A SOC is a decision system—not three queues stacked together.

People, telemetry, procedure, response authority, business context, and feedback must operate as one system. A major incident can bypass ordinary tier order when urgency or impact requires it.

Role ladder

Four views of the same incident

Select a role to see its mission, evidence standard, operating questions, handoff, and definition of success.

01

Mission

Determine whether the alert deserves investigation—and preserve the context Tier 2 will need.

Evidence standard

Original alert and event references, UTC time, device, identity, process lineage, source health, actions already taken, rationale, confidence, and open questions.

Questions to ask

  • Is the record valid and the source healthy?
  • Is the behavior expected for this user and asset?
  • Did execution occur or was it only attempted?
  • Is there file, DNS, network, identity, or registry corroboration?
  • Is the threat still active?

Handoff

Escalate when the threat is credible and investigation needs exceed initial-triage time, permissions, or technical scope. State exactly what remains unknown.

Success

A timely, accurately classified, evidence-rich escalation—or a documented benign disposition supported by evidence. Alert closure volume alone is not success.

02

Mission

Reconstruct what happened, determine compromise and scope, and support authorized containment.

Evidence standard

A sourced timeline across EDR, Sysmon, identity, email, network, file, registry, and sandbox evidence—with provenance and visibility gaps.

Questions to ask

  • Which process created and loaded the file?
  • Which behavior was observed versus merely possible?
  • Can network activity be attributed to the process?
  • Which other identities or devices show matching behavior?
  • What containment action is safe and authorized?

Handoff

Deliver the incident timeline, affected assets, confirmed and suspected behaviors, evidence sources, containment state, scope assessment, and collection gaps.

Success

A defensible compromise determination, contained threat, scoped environment, preserved evidence, and clear escalation for advanced analysis or engineering.

03

Mission

Understand the mechanism and build defenses that survive simple attacker changes.

Evidence standard

Reverse-engineering findings linked to observed telemetry, hunting logic, data dependencies, test results, tuning decisions, rollback, and ownership.

Questions to ask

  • Would the detection survive a new hash, name, path, or domain?
  • Does an API import represent capability or observed execution?
  • Which telemetry is required and how is its health monitored?
  • What legitimate workflows resemble the behavior?
  • Can the prevention control be staged and rolled back?

Handoff

Provide a reverse-engineering report, validated detection and hunting package, tested prevention proposal, and response-playbook update.

Success

Repeatable coverage for an attack class—not a one-sample rule—plus documented limitations, test evidence, deployment plan, and owner.

04

Management oversight

Ensure the right work is performed by qualified people, with the required urgency, authority, documentation, and measurable result.

Seven responsibilities

  1. Workforce and shift coverage
  2. Incident prioritization
  3. Containment authority and coordination
  4. Investigation quality assurance
  5. Performance and coverage
  6. Business and executive communication
  7. Security-engineering governance

Questions to ask

  • Who owns this incident and what decision is due next?
  • Does the evidence justify the stated confidence and severity?
  • Is business-critical context changing the response?
  • What authority is needed for the proposed action?
  • Which corrective actions will prevent recurrence?

Management output

Priorities, staffing, decision records, response authority, stakeholder updates, risk acceptance or escalation, engineering backlog, and closure evidence.

Success

Real threats are found, damage is limited, operations recover safely, stakeholders understand the risk, and validated improvements remain effective over time.

Authority, permissions, and automation

Give responders enough authority to act—without making every action unrestricted.

Microsoft Sentinel access model

Roles describe platform permission—not seniority.

  • Reader: view data, incidents, workbooks, and resources.
  • Responder: Reader permissions plus incident management.
  • Contributor: broader Sentinel content and resource management.
  • Playbook Operator: manually run authorized playbooks.
  • Automation Contributor: service permission for automation-rule playbook execution.

Logic Apps uses separate permissions. Role assignments are cumulative, so management must review the complete access path rather than one role name.

Automate enrichment freely. Automate disruption carefully.

Audience-aware communication

Four reports. One incident. Different decisions.

A strong manager preserves the facts and uncertainty while changing the technical depth for the audience.

T1 → T2Escalation

“FIN-WS-042 shows Word spawning encoded PowerShell followed by DNS activity. Execution is suspicious, but payload delivery and full behavior remain unconfirmed. Investigate process lineage, file staging, and network activity.”

T2 → Manager / T3Incident findings

“The endpoint shows DLL execution, Run-key persistence, and recurring network communication. Compromise is supported. Exfiltration and lateral movement are not established. Containment and enterprise scoping are required.”

T3 → ManagerEngineering assessment

“Analysis supports persistent network-worker behavior and task retrieval. We recommend behavioral coverage, Office child-process controls, DLL execution controls, and a tested response playbook. Production deployment still requires validation.”

SOC Manager → LeadershipBusiness update

“We confirmed a malware infection affecting a finance workstation. The endpoint is isolated, and the team is checking for additional affected systems. There is currently no confirmed evidence of data theft. Recovery and validated preventive improvements are in progress; the next update will follow the scoping decision.”

Executive briefing formula Confirmed facts+Unresolved risk+Actions taken+Decision or next update

Management acceptance criteria

A query is not a production detection.

  • Replayed malicious chain triggers expected logic
  • Benign Office workflow does not become a high-confidence incident
  • Renamed DLL and changed destination remain detectable
  • Missing telemetry produces a visible coverage warning
  • Automation follows approved authority and rollback
  • Rule has an owner, version, tuning history, and review date
