Operation Harbor Lantern

A plausible account compromise becomes a mail collection problem. The analyst must decide what the evidence proves, what remains uncertain, and which actions are justified now.

Fictional organizationSynthetic telemetryIdentity + emailNo actor attribution

Evidence boundary: Every event, time, account, domain, and organization below is invented for training. This is not reporting about a real intrusion or an assessment of activity in your environment.

Assessment

Likely account misuse

The sign-in and forwarding-rule sequence supports a working hypothesis of unauthorized mailbox access. The source of the credential remains unknown.

Confidence

Moderate

Two independent event types support misuse, but device, user-confirmation, and message-delivery evidence are incomplete.

Potential impact

Confidentiality risk

If the rule operated, future messages matching its scope could have reached an external destination. Actual delivery is unproven in this exercise.

01 / What is observed

Evidence timeline

08:42 · Sign-in event

A successful sign-in for the fictional procurement mailbox follows several failed attempts from a new network. The identity provider records an unfamiliar client fingerprint. This alone does not prove theft.

08:47 · Session activity

The same session opens mail settings. No endpoint telemetry is available for the client device.

08:51 · Rule creation

An audit event records a new forwarding rule targeting messages with vendor-invoice keywords and an external example-domain recipient.

09:06 · Analyst containment

The simulated response team disables the rule, revokes sessions, and starts a scoped review. The exercise does not supply proof that messages were forwarded.

02 / What is assessed

Competing explanations

H1 · Unauthorized use

Someone with access to the account created the rule to collect invoice-related mail. The unfamiliar sign-in, timing, and targeted external forwarding support this explanation.

Working assessment: most consistent with the supplied evidence.

H2 · Authorized automation

A user or approved workflow created the rule during legitimate vendor processing. This would explain the rule but needs owner confirmation and a documented business reason.

Test: confirm with the mailbox owner and change records.

H3 · Administrative activity

An administrator or delegated service acted on the mailbox. The provided audit set does not show a clear admin change, but collection is incomplete.

Test: review delegated access and admin audit logs.

Analytic judgment: Moderate confidence in unauthorized mailbox use, low confidence in how access was obtained, and no basis for naming a threat actor. No claim of successful exfiltration is made without message-trace evidence.

ATT&CK context

The scenario is consistent with T1078 Valid Accounts and T1114.003 Email Forwarding Rule. T1566 Phishing is a possible access path to investigate, not an observed fact here. ATT&CK labels describe behaviors; they do not identify the operator.

03 / What to do

Decisions tied to evidence

Now · Contain and preserve

  • Disable the suspicious rule and preserve its configuration and audit trail.
  • Revoke active sessions and reset credentials using the organization’s incident process.
  • Check MFA methods, recovery options, delegated access, and other mailbox rules.

Next 24 hours · Scope

  • Run message trace for messages that matched the rule and verify delivery status.
  • Search for related sign-ins and rule changes across accounts.
  • Ask the mailbox owner about legitimate automation and recent sign-in activity.

Next 30 days · Reduce recurrence

  • Alert on new external forwarding rules and unusual sign-in plus mail-setting sequences.
  • Review external forwarding policy and exceptions.
  • Exercise a response playbook and test that logs contain the needed fields.

Likely business impact: invoice and supplier correspondence could expose payment details, negotiations, and contact information if matching mail was delivered externally. The exercise does not establish any financial loss.

04 / Method and sources

How to read this exercise

Observed facts are synthetic event records described above. Assessments are reasoned interpretations with confidence and collection gaps. Recommended actions are examples for a defensive team to adapt to its environment. The scenario is not a current threat bulletin.
